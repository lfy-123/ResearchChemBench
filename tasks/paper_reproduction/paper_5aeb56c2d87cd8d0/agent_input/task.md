# Scientific objective

Independently test the authors' qualitative hypothesis that the 1:1 complex of receptor L1 with ibuprofen anion in DMSO is organized by the squaramide donor site binding the carboxylate, with additional contact between aromatic regions. Determine whether a defensible solution-phase structure search supports a bound complex, characterize the lowest accessible binding motifs, calculate a DMSO binding energy, and determine the direction of the lowest-energy UV-visible absorption-band shift on complexation.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

The complete identities, charge, multiplicity, stoichiometry, solvent, temperature, and stereochemical boundary are in `data/inputs/system.json`. Generate all three-dimensional structures independently from those identities. The research object is the isolated 1:1 anionic complex `[L1·IBU]−` and the isolated neutral L1 and IBU anion reference fragments in DMSO; sodium and other counterions are outside scope. Do not infer enantioselectivity from the unspecified ibuprofen stereocenter.

Report (i) distinct optimized complex candidates and their relative solution-phase thermochemistry, (ii) the binding geometry and intermolecular contacts of the selected low-energy candidate or ensemble, (iii) the solution-phase binding energy in kJ mol−1 defined as `E(complex) - E(L1) - E(IBU_anion)` using consistently optimized lowest reference structures and one stated solvation/electronic-structure convention, with any deformation, BSSE, thermal, or standard-state corrections separately identified, and (iv) the signed shift in nm of the lowest-energy intense absorption feature of the complex relative to free L1 under one consistent excited-state protocol. A positive signed shift means a shift to longer wavelength.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Generate a diverse but finite set of L1 conformers, ibuprofen-anion conformers, and 1:1 encounter geometries that varies guest orientation, receptor torsions, and chemically distinct contact motifs. Deduplicate candidates by connectivity, intermolecular contact pattern, and geometry; retain stable alternatives rather than only the best initial guess. Advance candidates using a documented energy/geometry criterion. Optimize the finalists in a DMSO solution model and verify each reported minimum by vibrational analysis or a comparably direct minimum test. Re-evaluate the leading ordering with at least one defensible sensitivity check, such as a higher-quality energy calculation, altered solvation treatment, or expanded conformer search. Calculate comparable spectra for free L1 and the selected complex candidate or thermally weighted ensemble.

The investigation is complete when newly generated, nonduplicate motifs no longer change the leading binding motif, binding-energy sign, or spectral-shift direction within the stated uncertainty; the reported low-energy candidates have passed the minimum test; and at least one sensitivity check has been completed. Stop when this criterion is met or when the declared compute limit is exhausted. In the latter case, submit `bounded_failure`, preserve all computed evidence, identify which criterion remains unresolved, and do not fabricate a numeric result.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to `submission_schema.json`. It must identify methods and conventions; enumerate each advanced complex candidate with geometry identity, contact description, and minimum-validation evidence, and with relative energy plus its definition for a resolved investigation; document deduplication, coverage, completion status, limitations, and the required sensitivity check when resolved; and state the selected candidate or ensemble identifiers, binding-energy result and corrections, assigned absorption feature and shift, leading motif, and scientific conclusion when resolved. A bounded-failure submission may report an empty candidate set or partial candidates without success-only numerical fields, but it must identify the unresolved criteria and preserve every result actually obtained. Geometry identifiers may be paths or hashes for retained structures; the JSON is the only required artifact.
