# Scientific objective

Independently test the authors' qualitative proposal that THF C–H borylation by the supplied L8-derived anionic iridium trisboryl catalyst proceeds through competing α and β C–H oxidative-addition channels followed by rearrangement and C–B reductive elimination. Compute a defensible solution-phase free-energy profile and determine whether the calculated barriers support the experimentally observed α-selective THF borylation and C–H cleavage as the likely turnover-limiting event. Report activation free energies in kcal/mol relative to the supplied catalyst ion-pair reference plus free THF under one consistent convention.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

`data/inputs/catalyst_1.xyz` contains the 91 atoms of the singlet sodium ion pair of the L8-derived anionic Ir(III) trisboryl complex: the Ir complex has formal charge −1 and the file includes one Na+ counterion. `thf.xyz` is neutral tetrahydrofuran, and `b2pin2.xyz` is neutral bis(pinacolato)diboron. Coordinates are Cartesian Å. Preserve each file's atom ordering when reporting mappings; coordinate atom numbers are the one-based coordinate-line indices after the two-line XYZ header.

The modeled reaction boundary is the supplied catalyst ion pair plus THF at 373.15 K, with THF serving as both solvent and substrate. B2pin2 supplies catalytic context but is not part of the four scored elementary-event barriers. If a different counterion placement, explicit-solvent cluster, or separated-ion treatment is used, justify it and preserve a comparable common reference. Treat α as reaction at either symmetry-equivalent THF carbon directly bonded to the ring oxygen; treat β as reaction at either symmetry-equivalent ring carbon one C–C bond farther from oxygen. An oxidative-addition event cleaves the selected THF C–H bond while forming Ir–H and Ir–C interactions. A reductive-elimination event forms the C–B bond at the correspondingly defined α or β carbon from an assigned downstream Ir intermediate. These connectivity definitions, rather than paper-internal structure labels, determine event identity.

The measured quantities are validated stationary points and activation free energies for α and β C–H oxidative addition and α and β C–B reductive elimination, their ordering where supported, and a bounded mechanistic interpretation. The authors' qualitative route may guide candidate generation, but no paper numerical result, selected transition-state or intermediate geometry, software, model chemistry, ordered protocol, result ordering, or tolerance is supplied or required.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose and justify an independent computational approach suitable for a large anionic organoiridium ion pair. Generate and deduplicate plausible α and β candidates, including relevant substrate approaches, ion-pair arrangements, and conformers. For every candidate, retain a unique ID, target event, starting-geometry provenance, atom mapping and connectivity-based event definition, computational method, disposition, and candidate-specific validation evidence.

Advance only candidates with converged optimizations and a chemically assigned stationary point. Validate minima with zero imaginary frequencies and transition states with exactly one chemically relevant imaginary mode whose displacement corresponds to the assigned C–H oxidative addition or C–B reductive elimination; an IRC or endpoint-displacement optimization may supplement the mode assignment. Report unsuccessful searches rather than silently replacing them. Construct all free energies with a common reference and explicitly report the electronic-energy method, thermal correction and temperature, solvation treatment, standard state, THF-as-substrate concentration/activity treatment, and any ion-pair correction. Compare α and β only when these conventions are compatible.

The investigation is complete when all four named events have either at least one validated candidate or a documented bounded failure, all advanced candidates have individual validation evidence, and further candidates would duplicate an already sampled structural/mechanistic class or require a stated expansion beyond the chosen coverage. Stop at that coverage criterion. Report conformational, ion-pair, method, solvation, concentration, and transition-state uncertainties and do not claim an unsearched space is exhaustive.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to `submission_schema.json`. It must contain the candidate-level audit trail; one explicit outcome branch for each of the four events; free-energy bookkeeping; comparisons supported by validated events; and conclusions about turnover limitation and selectivity whose strength is limited by the achieved coverage.

A `validated` event must identify its supporting candidate(s), provide its numeric activation free energy, and summarize stationary-point evidence. A `bounded_failure` event must instead identify attempted candidates, failure causes, sampled coverage, and a concrete next step; it must not fabricate a barrier. The overall `complete` status is reserved for four validated events. Use `partial_bounded_failure` if one or more events use the bounded-failure branch. A parseable report satisfying the appropriate branches and the stated evidence requirements is the completion criterion.
