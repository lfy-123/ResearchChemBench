# Scientific objective

Independently test the authors' qualitative proposal that a base-promoted intramolecular hydrogen migration creates an allylic bromide intermediate and that acetate then performs remote migratory nucleophilic substitution. Starting from the public substrate, acetate, base, solvent and product connectivity, construct and validate a defensible computational free-energy profile. Report the relative free energies of the relevant intermediates you actually locate, the lowest validated productive transition-state barrier, and the product free energy relative to your stated reference. The author hypothesis is a route to test, not a prescribed structure or numerical answer.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

`data/inputs/system.json` defines the unique substrate, its Z alkene stereochemistry, neutral singlet state, acetic acid/acetate under cesium-carbonate basic conditions, acetonitrile environment, temperature and the experimentally known product connectivity. The explicit acetate/cesium species are a charge-balanced computational representation of the nucleophile generated under those conditions; do not simultaneously count the acetate anion and the acetic-acid equivalent as two independent nucleophile equivalents. You may generate conformers and working geometries. Treat the molecular identities, charge and multiplicity in that file as fixed, while documenting any proton-transfer model used to connect the experimental acid/base mixture to the chosen computational reference. The scope is molecular quantum chemistry with an implicit acetonitrile model and a relative-free-energy convention that you must state. The measured quantities are stationary-point frequencies/connectivity, relative free energies in kcal/mol, and the activation free energy from the selected reactant reference to the validated productive transition state. No paper method, software, geometry, energy, ranking or tolerance is supplied.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose and justify an electronic-structure, solvation, thermal and standard-state approach suitable for this charged, conformationally flexible reaction. Plan and execute a search over plausible conformers and at least two chemically distinct realizations of the proposed hydrogen-migration/acetate-substitution route. Give every attempted candidate a stable identity, role/family, input-geometry provenance and atom mapping; deduplicate equivalent structures using a stated structural criterion. Advance a candidate only when optimization converges and its connectivity is chemically assigned. Validate each claimed minimum by absence of imaginary frequencies. Validate each claimed transition state by exactly one imaginary frequency and an IRC, relaxed scan, or equivalent endpoint/connectivity check whose two endpoint identities are reported for that candidate. Use a consistent method/solvation/thermal convention, document uncertainty and sensitivity checks where affordable, and report every rejected candidate and its rejection reason. The investigation is complete when every retained stationary point is validated, the productive pathway is connected from the stated reference to the known product, and further reasonable starting guesses produce no new low-lying validated pathway. If no fully connected productive pathway is obtained, stop at a declared computational or coverage bound and submit the bounded-failure branch with all attempts, validation outcomes, stopping evidence and limitations; do not invent barrier or product-energy values.

# Deliverables

Submit the files declared in `submission_schema.json`. `report/results.json` must use either the `successful_profile` or `bounded_failure` outcome branch. Both branches require the chosen computational approach, reference definition, object-specific candidate inventory and validation evidence, rejected attempts, search coverage, uncertainty/limitations and a conclusion. The successful branch additionally requires a connected pathway, activation free energy and known-product relative free energy. Include enough computational provenance to reproduce the actual investigation.
