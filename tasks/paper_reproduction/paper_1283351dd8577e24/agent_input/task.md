# Scientific objective

Determine, for neutral enol 7,9-bis(4-nitrophenyl)-10-hydroxybenzo[h]quinoline (1NO2), whether the lowest singlet excited-state potential along the hydroxy O–H distance contains a distinct basin before proton transfer, and use validated calculations to characterize the basin, the rise toward transfer, and the electronic/structural reason for the behavior. The authors qualitatively proposed that strong electron-withdrawing substituents can redirect the excited-state charge transfer away from the HBq proton-acceptor unit; independently test that hypothesis rather than assuming it is correct.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/molecular_identity.json` and `data/inputs/problem_boundary.json`. The object is the neutral, closed-shell enol molecule specified there, not a salt, aggregate, protonated ion, deuterated analogue, or keto starting tautomer. The solvent boundary is dichloromethane and the reaction coordinate is the distance from the hydroxy oxygen to its attached hydrogen, in Å. Investigate S0 and the lowest singlet excited state S1 of the isolated molecule. You may construct 3-D coordinates and computational models independently. Do not use the paper, SI, general web, or unpinned literature as input; only the supplied identity/boundary files and your chosen software/database resources are allowed. Do not treat a failed optimization as a numerical result.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Generate at least two chemically distinct starting conformers by varying the two aryl/backbone torsions, retain their atom mapping and optimization outcomes, and advance a conformer only after checking connectivity, charge, multiplicity, absence of imaginary modes where applicable, and convergence. For the selected conformer, obtain an S1 O–H profile with enough points to establish the local shape and the direction toward proton transfer; state the sampled range and spacing and explain any adaptive refinement. For every profile point report the constrained O–H distance, relative energy, state definition, convergence status and geometry identifier. Validate the candidate basin against neighboring points and at least one independent check (for example a denser local scan, alternative starting geometry, or independently converged optimization). Examine the S1 geometry and transition/orbital or density evidence needed to discriminate HBq-local charge transfer from substituent-localized charge transfer. The investigation is complete when the chosen conformer, a converged profile covering both the intact-hydrogen-bond and transfer-side regions, the basin/barrier analysis, and the electronic interpretation are all documented. Stop when further refinement changes the reported basin/barrier within the investigator's stated uncertainty, or stop earlier only with a scientifically justified failure/coverage limitation; report all failed candidates and remaining ambiguity.
