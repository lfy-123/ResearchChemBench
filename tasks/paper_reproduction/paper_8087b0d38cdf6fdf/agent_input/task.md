# Scientific objective

Test the authors' proposed UV-induced intramolecular photocyclization of tetraphenylethylene derivative 1. Starting from the supplied open reactant, independently construct the chemically plausible cyclized stereoisomers, optimize and validate them, and determine the relative ground-state electronic energies of the open reactant and each distinct product. In this reproduction mode, the qualitative author hypothesis is public: cyclization is proposed to produce E and Z stereoisomers and is suggested as the origin of the solid-state photochromism. Do not assume that this hypothesis is correct.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

The file `data/inputs/compound_1.xyz` is the complete Cartesian geometry of compound 1, with 82 atoms, coordinates in Å, formula C44H31NO6, charge 0 and multiplicity 1. It is the only molecular starting structure. Treat the molecule as isolated and in its electronic ground state; solvent, crystal packing, intermolecular interactions, and excited-state dynamics are outside the required calculation. The measured quantities are optimized-structure identity, stationary-point evidence, electronic energies, and energy differences in kJ mol−1 relative to the open reactant. A “product” means a covalent intramolecular cyclization of the supplied molecule that forms one new C–C bond between the two aryl termini implicated by the proposed TPE photocyclization while retaining atom count and formula. E/Z labels must be defined from the relative sides of the two newly bonded stereogenic hydrogen substituents, and any alternative naming must retain a unique structure file and atom mapping.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Generate a finite, chemically justified set of distinct open and cyclized candidates, record how candidates were generated, and deduplicate by connectivity and stereochemistry (not by label alone). Optimize every advanced candidate with a defensible electronic-structure method, verify charge, multiplicity, formula/atom conservation and connectivity, and provide stationary-point evidence (frequency analysis or an explicitly justified equivalent). Use one common energy convention and reference all differences to the optimized open reactant. Investigate both stereochemical outcomes if chemically accessible and report failed or non-stationary candidates rather than silently dropping them. Completion requires either (a) a validated set covering all distinct products generated by the defined cyclization bond and stereochemical alternatives, or (b) a bounded-failure report that identifies the unresolved candidates and gives reproducible reasons. Stop when that coverage/completion condition is met; do not claim exhaustive discovery beyond the stated generation rule. Compare the resulting ordering and energy profile with the qualitative author hypothesis and the published barrier range only in the final analysis, clearly distinguishing an energy difference from a transition-state activation barrier.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` and supporting files referenced from it. The JSON must include candidate identities and structure-file paths, per-candidate charge/multiplicity, optimization and stationary-point validation, absolute and relative energies with units and reference definition, candidate-generation and deduplication record, coverage/completion status, comparison with the qualitative hypothesis, barrier-range comparison if computed, limitations, and a concise final conclusion. Include enough calculation metadata and raw output paths for another researcher to reproduce the investigation. If bounded failure is reported, include the unresolved candidate identities, attempted validations, and the scientific stopping rationale.
