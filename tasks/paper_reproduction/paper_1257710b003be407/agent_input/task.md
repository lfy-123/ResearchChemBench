# Scientific objective

Independently test the authors' qualitative proposal that a Cu(I)/L1 bromide complex can activate the supplied substituted propargylic bromide by halogen-atom transfer (XAT), and determine the activation Gibbs free energy of the lowest defensible XAT transition state at 298.15 K in DMF. The research object is the explicitly supplied Cu/L1/Br assembly plus (3-bromo-5-phenylpent-1-yn-1-yl)trimethylsilane; the measured quantities are stationary-point character, reactant/transition-state connectivity, and ΔG‡ in kcal/mol. The author hypothesis is qualitative only: evaluate an XAT/radical route involving the Cu(I) complex, rather than assuming a concerted polar substitution route.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/cu_l1_br_int1.xyz` as the starting Cu(I)/L1/bromide assembly (Cartesian Å; element labels are explicit) and the substituted propargylic bromide with the self-contained SMILES `C[Si](C)(C)C#CC(Br)CCc1ccccc1` (formula C14H19BrSi, 35 atoms including explicit hydrogens). This substrate is not the unsubstituted compound `C#CCBr`. The supplied complex is neutral, singlet (`charge=0`, `multiplicity=1`); the organic bromide is neutral singlet. The substrate SMILES leaves its stereocenter unspecified: report which configurations and conformers you investigate rather than silently claiming exhaustive stereochemical coverage. DMF and 298.15 K define the solvent and temperature boundary. You may build a reactant encounter complex and generate 3-D structures, but must report the exact structures, atom mapping, charge, multiplicity and conformers used. Do not use the paper, SI, general web, or hidden reference structures. The task scores the XAT endpoint and does not require a full catalytic-cycle model or a product yield.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose and document a defensible computational model and search protocol independently. Generate a finite, explicitly described set of reactant assemblies and XAT transition-state candidates; deduplicate candidates by mapped connectivity and structural similarity; advance only converged candidates. For each advanced candidate report optimization convergence, charge/multiplicity, all relevant bond changes and frequency results. A valid TS has exactly one imaginary frequency whose mode involves C–Br cleavage/halogen transfer and has two-sided IRC (or a scientifically justified equivalent) connecting the stated reactant and post-XAT product valleys. A valid reactant reference is a converged local minimum. Calculate and show how ΔG‡ is formed in DMF at 298.15 K, including standard-state and thermal choices. Completion requires either one validated XAT TS with a numeric ΔG‡ and traceable evidence, or a bounded-failure report listing every attempted candidate, why validation failed, and the next limitation. Stop after the documented candidate-generation space has been exhausted or after additional independent searches cease to produce a new validated XAT connectivity; in either case report coverage and stopping rationale.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` and any supporting files under `report/`. The JSON must identify the selected reactant and TS structures, preserve atom mapping, state charge/multiplicity and method/software, give frequency and connectivity validation, give ΔG‡ in kcal/mol when a validated TS exists, and include a final conclusion, limitations, and a `coverage_stopping` account. If no validated TS is obtained, use the bounded-failure branch: do not fabricate a TS or barrier; list every attempted candidate with its failure reason and give the next limitation. Do not report paper reference values or claim agreement without an independently computed value.
