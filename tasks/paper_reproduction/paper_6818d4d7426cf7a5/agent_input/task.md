# Scientific objective

Determine, by an independent computational investigation, whether the two competing anomeric radical-addition transition states in the supplied Ni-catalyzed decarboxylative glycosyl C–Ge coupling have different activation Gibbs free energies, and use that comparison to test the origin of the observed α/β selectivity. The authors qualitatively propose catalyst-organized radical addition to a Ni(I) complex; test this hypothesis independently. Do not assume or copy any paper numerical result or optimized transition-state geometry.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

The public inputs are `data/inputs/glycosyl_nhpi_22c.xyz` (45-atom glycosyl NHPI ester model, neutral singlet), `data/inputs/ni0_ligand_complex.xyz` (29-atom Ni(0)L model, neutral singlet), and `data/inputs/int1_reference_precursor.xyz` (51-atom radical-addition precursor model, neutral singlet). XYZ coordinates are in Å; element symbols are explicit and atom order is fixed within each file. Combine or transform these structures only when chemically justified and document every transformation, charge and multiplicity. The target states are the two distinct anomeric radical-addition pathways from the same defined precursor; their product stereochemistry, optimized geometries and energies are not public. Compute activation free energies at 298.15 K relative to one common reactant/reference state, in kcal mol−1, using a defensible electronic-structure and thermochemical treatment of your choice. Solvent/environment treatment and model truncations must be reported. The experimental boundary is qualitative observed α-selectivity; it is not a numerical target.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Generate and retain a finite, chemically distinct set of candidate structures for each of the two possible anomeric additions. Deduplicate by connectivity and stereochemical identity, and record how candidates were generated and screened. Optimize or otherwise converge each advanced candidate, verify the intended bond-making/connectivity change and transition-state character (for example by frequencies and an appropriate reaction-coordinate test), and reject or flag structures that fail. Establish that both pathways start from the same reference convention, and report conformer/search coverage, failed attempts and method sensitivity. The investigation is complete when each pathway has either a validated best candidate with a defensible barrier and uncertainty estimate, or an explicitly documented bounded failure to locate one. Stop when additional candidate generation under the stated protocol yields no chemically distinct validated improvement or when the stated computational/resource boundary is reached; report the stopping reason and limitations.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to `submission_schema.json`. Include candidate identities and validation evidence for both pathways, the reference-state convention, barriers and units when available, the barrier difference and direction, uncertainty/coverage, and a final mechanistic conclusion. A bounded-failure branch is allowed only with the required failure explanation and attempted-search record. Also include concise provenance in the JSON; do not include paper text or evaluator files.
