# Scientific objective

Determine the six lowest vertical electronic excitations of the neutral doublet 2-methyl-1-propenyl radical (C4H7) from the supplied ground-state geometry, reporting each excitation wavelength in nm, oscillator strength, and dominant electronic character. Independently test the authors' qualitative proposal that a catalyst-free radical's UV feature is associated primarily with a 3s Rydberg excitation and possibly an n→π* excitation, and assess whether the computed spectrum is consistent with the observed H-atom PFY and product dynamics. Do not assume the authors' numerical values or computational protocol.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/2_methyl_1_propenyl_doublet.xyz`: its 11 atoms are ordered C,C,H,H,H,C,H,H,H,C,H, coordinates are in Å, charge is 0 and multiplicity is 2. Use `problem_boundary.json` for the measured PFY window (226–248 nm), broad PFY feature near 240 nm with increased intensity below 228 nm, broad product translational-energy feature near 7 kcal mol−1, and approximately isotropic H-atom angular distribution. The object is the isolated gas-phase radical at a vertical-excitation geometry; no solvent, counterion, precursor, or product structure is part of the computational object. State labels D1–D6 must be assigned in increasing computed excitation energy within the reported calculation.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose and justify an independent electronic-structure route capable of producing at least six doublet excited states. Establish geometry and electronic-state provenance, verify the input charge/multiplicity and atom count, and report convergence or an explicitly bounded failure. For every D1–D6 retain wavelength, oscillator strength, state identity and evidence for its dominant character (for example NTOs, orbital analysis, or a clearly justified alternative). Check sensitivity to at least one defensible method, basis, or geometry choice and state whether any state identity or ordering changes. Compare the calculated wavelengths/intensities with the 226–248 nm PFY boundary and explain what the low-energy translational distribution and isotropic angular distribution do or do not support about internal conversion and subsequent ground-state dissociation. Assess the disclosed author hypothesis, including contrary or inconclusive evidence. Completion requires either a validated six-state table plus interpretation or a bounded-failure report identifying the failed step, partial evidence, missing evidence and one scientifically justified recovery attempt. Stop when the six-state calculation, character assignment, sensitivity check and measurement comparison are complete; if recovery fails, stop after documenting it rather than silently substituting values.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to `submission_schema.json`. In it, declare paths to machine-readable calculation outputs or logs and a concise methods/validation narrative. A complete result must include geometry provenance, charge/multiplicity, the chosen approach and justification, all six states, uncertainty/sensitivity information, measurement comparison, limitations, and a final assessment of the qualitative author hypothesis. A bounded failure must instead truthfully report the failed step, available partial results, recovery attempt, missing evidence and stopping rationale; do not fabricate a six-state table. Numeric results must be your computed results, not copied reference values.
