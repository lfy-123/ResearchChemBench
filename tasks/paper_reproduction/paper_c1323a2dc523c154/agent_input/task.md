# Scientific objective

Independently test the authors' qualitative hypothesis that the neutral singlet Pd2Cl6 unit has a structured Pd 4d-to-2p X-ray emission spectrum whose lower-energy shoulder is produced by several Pd–Cl-involving valence transitions. Compute the spectrum, identify its reproducible features, and decide whether your calculations support that hypothesis. Do not assume a feature location, intensity, or orbital assignment in advance.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/pd2cl6_neutral_singlet.xyz`, an eight-atom fixed starting geometry containing two Pd and six Cl atoms. Treat the molecular unit as total charge 0 and multiplicity 1. The research object is this isolated molecular unit; a periodic crystal, counterions, solvent molecules, and thermal disorder are outside scope. The target observable is nonresonant Pd 4d-to-2p emission from occupied valence levels into Pd 2p core holes. Report transition energies and intensities, a normalized broadened spectrum over a window wide enough to contain the complete main line and its low-energy side, and the orbital/interaction character of transitions that form distinct features. Absolute energy alignment may use a stated defensible calibration or shift, but raw and aligned energies must remain distinguishable.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose an electronic-structure and XES treatment suitable for Pd, explicitly documenting relativistic treatment, basis/ECP choices, charge, multiplicity, and emission-intensity formalism. Validate the supplied geometry by either optimizing it and checking that it is a minimum, or by demonstrating that a constrained/fixed-geometry spectrum is insensitive to a separately optimized structure. Report Pd–Pd and all six nearest Pd–Cl distances for the final spectrum geometry. Retain a transition table with stable transition IDs, raw energy, aligned energy, intensity, donor-orbital identity, and quantitative or well-supported qualitative Pd d and Cl p character. Show that the plotted spectrum can be regenerated from that table and state the broadening kernel and width. Test at least one scientifically meaningful sensitivity affecting feature identity (geometry, broadening, energy alignment, or electronic-structure model), and distinguish robust assignments from model-dependent ones.

Completion requires a converged, physically interpretable calculation; geometry validation; a transition-resolved spectrum; identification of the principal feature and any distinct lower-energy shoulder; and a supported verdict on the stated hypothesis. Stop when additional refinement no longer changes the existence and assignment of reported features within your declared resolution. If convergence or XES evaluation remains impossible after two materially different defensible attempts, stop as a bounded failure and report both attempts, diagnostics, partial transition/geometry evidence, and the strongest conclusion that the evidence permits without fabricating spectral values.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to the supplied schema. It must identify the exact input, computational model, outcome status, geometry validation, transition provenance, spectrum construction, sensitivity test, feature measurements, hypothesis verdict, and limitations. For successful work include the complete transition array and sampled normalized spectrum. For bounded failure include the attempted models, diagnostics, available geometry/transition evidence, and a non-fabricated limitation conclusion.
