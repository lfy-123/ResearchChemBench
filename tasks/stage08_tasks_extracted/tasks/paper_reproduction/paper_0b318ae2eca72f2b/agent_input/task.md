# Scientific objective

Determine, by independent computational investigation, which oxygen environment and proton configuration is most favorable in monoclinic C2/m VO2(B) at H0.25VO2(B), and whether the intermediate composition is energetically favorable relative to pristine VO2(B) and a stated hydrogen reference.

## Author-provided scientific guidance

**Author hypothesis or claim.**
The authors propose that the oxygen local environment, especially its degree of transition-metal coordination, controls the ease of proton intercalation. For this task, test the qualitative claim that the less transition-metal-coordinated oxygen environment is the preferred proton-binding environment.

**Candidate route or mechanism.**
Include proton configurations at the crystallographically distinct oxygen environments and their symmetry-equivalent placements, with particular attention to configurations that place protons at oxygen environments differing in transition-metal coordination. Treat this as a proposed site preference to compare across the generated configurations rather than as a predetermined result.

**Discriminating evidence.**
Use consistently relaxed structures and internally consistent insertion or formation energies to compare the candidate site/configuration classes. Support the interpretation with the reported oxygen identities, proton placement geometries, convergence and structural validation, and comparison against pristine VO2(B) and the stated hydrogen reference.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/vo2b_pristine.cif`, which defines the conventional C2/m VO2(B) cell, its lattice, symmetry, asymmetric V1/V2/O1/O2/O3/O4 identities and fractional coordinates. Use `data/inputs/problem_definition.json`: neutral periodic H0.25VO2(B), two inserted H atoms in the Z=8 cell. Do not use the paper, SI or general web. You may choose software, functional, pseudopotential, spin state, k-point sampling and relaxation settings, but report them. The scored research object is the relaxed candidate site/configuration and its energy relative to the consistently defined pristine and hydrogen references; no transition state or product structure is requested.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Propose a finite, scientifically justified candidate-generation rule covering the distinct oxygen environments and symmetry-equivalent placements that could accommodate two H atoms. Retain unique candidate identities and placement coordinates, deduplicate by symmetry/geometry, and report coverage. Advance each retained candidate through the same optimization and verify force/energy convergence, cell integrity, composition, charge and absence of atom loss. Compute an internally consistent formation or insertion energy for every advanced candidate and explain the reference convention. Completion requires either a converged comparison of all candidates generated by the stated rule or a bounded-failure report identifying the unresolved candidates and why. Stop when the proposed generation rule is exhausted and all retained candidates have been compared; if resources prevent this, stop at the declared resource boundary and report coverage and limitations. State and discriminate at least one plausible alternative explanation for the observed energetics if the data support one.

# Deliverables

Submit `report/results.json` conforming to `submission_schema.json`. Include the candidate table, selected candidate identity, energies, convergence/validation evidence, coverage and stopping status, competing interpretation, plus a concise conclusion. Include coordinates or structure files for the selected and compared candidates when available. A bounded-failure branch is allowed only if it truthfully records the completed subset and unresolved scope.
