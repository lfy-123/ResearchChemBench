# Scientific objective

Determine, by independent computational investigation, how acetate converts the public brominated diene substrate into the public conjugated dienyl acetate in acetonitrile under basic conditions. Identify and discriminate plausible intermediates and transition states, construct the best-supported relative free-energy profile, and report the activation free energy and product free energy relative to a clearly defined reference. Do not assume a mechanism in advance; if multiple explanations remain viable, compare them and state what evidence separates or fails to separate them.

## Author-provided scientific guidance

**Author hypothesis or claim.**
The authors propose that bromine influences the substrate electronic distribution and enables a base-promoted remote migratory process. In their interpretation, the transformation proceeds through formation of a brominated conjugated diene, followed by incorporation of carboxylate to give the conjugated dienyl ester.

**Candidate route or mechanism.**
A candidate route to test is intramolecular hydrogen migration from the brominated penta-1,4-diene to form a 5-bromopenta-1,3-diene intermediate, followed by remote migratory nucleophilic substitution in which acetate replaces the migrating allylic bromide arrangement and furnishes the observed dienyl acetate connectivity. The search may compare this stepwise sequence with alternative ordering or concerted realizations of hydrogen transfer, bromide departure, and acetate bond formation.

**Discriminating evidence.**
Use optimized stationary points, frequency and connectivity assignments, transition-state validation with endpoint or IRC evidence, and relative free-energy comparisons to test whether the proposed brominated diene and acetate-incorporation events are chemically accessible and connected to the known product. Compare competing pathways by their computed barriers, intermediate stability, conformational requirements, and atom mappings; report evidence that supports or fails to distinguish the proposal.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Choose and justify an electronic-structure, solvation, thermal and standard-state approach suitable for this charged, conformationally flexible reaction. Before selecting a route, formulate a finite set of chemically distinct candidate mechanism families consistent with the public reactants and endpoint; the set must include credible alternatives in bond-making/bond-breaking order, proton transfer, substitution/elimination and conformational organization, without privileging a paper-derived family. Retain stable candidate IDs, family/role assignments, atom mappings, geometry provenance and the reason each candidate was generated. Deduplicate equivalent structures with a stated criterion and compare the families using their object-specific computed evidence. A candidate may advance only after converged optimization and chemical connectivity assignment. Validate minima by no imaginary frequencies; validate transition states by exactly one imaginary frequency plus IRC, relaxed scan, or equivalent endpoint/connectivity evidence whose two endpoint identities are reported for that candidate. Use a consistent computational/thermal convention, quantify uncertainty and perform sensitivity checks where feasible, and report rejected candidates and unsearched alternatives. Stop when all declared families are covered and additional independent seeds yield no new validated productive pathway within the declared search scope. If that cannot be achieved, use the bounded-failure branch with attempts, validation, stopping evidence and limitations rather than inventing a mechanism or numerical profile.

# Deliverables

Submit the files declared in `submission_schema.json`. `report/results.json` must use either the `successful_profile` or `bounded_failure` outcome branch. Both branches require the chosen computational approach, independently formulated mechanism families, object-specific candidate/validation records, rejected attempts, reference definition, uncertainty, coverage/stopping evidence and a conclusion. The successful branch additionally requires a connected pathway, activation free energy and known-product relative free energy. A successful conclusion must distinguish computed evidence from interpretation; bounded failure must not contain fabricated profile values.
