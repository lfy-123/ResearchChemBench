# Scientific objective

Independently compute the one-electron Pr(IV)/Pr(III) oxidation potential of the uniquely identified potassium-containing homoleptic complex K[Pr(NP(tBu)2(pyrrolidinyl))4], conventionally called 1-KPr(NPC2), on a Fc/Fc+ scale in THF. The measured quantity is the adiabatic Pr4+/Pr3+ potential in volts versus Fc/Fc+; the experimental boundary for discussion is the irreversible 1-KPr(NPC2) CV wave in THF. Formulate any mechanistic or counterion-retention interpretation from your own calculations and evidence; no author route or preferred explanation is supplied.

# Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/structure_record.json` and the controlled Figshare record DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30338485, selecting the entry labelled `1-KPr(NPC2)`. This is the only structure source permitted for the starting coordinates. The formula is K[Pr(NP(tBu)2(pyrrolidinyl))4], with four monoanionic NPC2 ligands, neutral total charge, Pr(III), and singlet multiplicity for the supplied starting state. Define and compare the chemically plausible oxidized counterion models supported by the record (at minimum an explicitly defined retained-K model and a cation-released model); do not assume which model is correct. Report and justify charge and multiplicity for every state. The thermodynamic model is molecular gas phase for optimization/frequencies and a stated solution-phase treatment in THF, with Fc/Fc+ as the reference. Explicit solvent and crystal packing are outside scope. The published experimental boundary is Epa = −0.60 V and Epc = −1.47 V versus Fc/Fc+ for the irreversible Pr4+/Pr3+ wave in THF; it is only a comparison boundary, not the requested theoretical target.

# Required scientific validation/investigation

Plan and execute an independent electronic-structure investigation that discriminates at least the chemically plausible oxidation-state/counterion models needed to support your conclusion. Generate only finite, connectivity-preserving candidates from the supplied record; deduplicate by connectivity, charge, multiplicity and retained/released-counterion identity; advance a candidate only when its geometry is converged and its electronic state is documented. For every advanced state, perform a stationary-point test (preferably frequencies), report imaginary modes if present, and compute the terms needed for an adiabatic potential and Fc/Fc+ reference. Report the candidate coverage, selection rationale, all model choices, convergence settings and limitations. The investigation is complete when every proposed model has either passed these validation gates or has a documented bounded failure, and one model has a reproducible potential or a scientifically justified inability to obtain one. Stop when that coverage and validation record is complete; do not continue an unbounded search or invent an unvalidated number.
