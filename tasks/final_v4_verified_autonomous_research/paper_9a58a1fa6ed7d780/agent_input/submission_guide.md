# Submission guide

Submit `report/results.json` and `report/report.md`. The JSON records your research question, chosen models, actual operations, quantitative results, evidence, decisions, claims, validation and limitations. IDs and branch names are yours. Hypotheses may be added, revised or withdrawn; there is no required hypothesis count, predetermined explanation, control count or calculation matrix. A sound empirical investigation need not invent named hypotheses.

`models` must link atom-resolved identity files and explain mappings, composition, charge, spin, conditions and assumptions. New conformers, states, intermediates or finite models are allowed when physically admissible within the task's chemical scope. An input graph defines starting chemical identity; it does not prescribe geometry, a preferred endpoint or the only valid model. Justify changes of composition or state.

`operations` records the method and actual input/parameter and execution-log files, linked models, artifacts and failures. `results` contains numerical quantities with units, reference/state/condition definitions, uncertainty and extraction locators into raw products. Do not place unrelated quantities into a convenient schema field. The `other` quantity type needs a precise quantity/unit definition and receives the same physical and unit checks as named types. Every result, model, operation, claim and artifact reference must resolve uniquely.

Keep inputs, native outputs, structures, analyses and execution records under `outputs/`, `structures/`, `analysis/` or `logs/`; list their actual SHA256 in `artifacts`. Analysis of public observations is permitted, but supplied observations must remain labeled public evidence rather than new calculations. Generated analyses still need reproducible code, inputs and execution records. A formatted table or self-authored log alone cannot establish that a scientific calculation happened.

`decisions` stores concise purpose/prediction, action, observed evidence and resulting continuation/revision linked to execution records. Do not submit hidden chain of thought. Self-authored timestamps do not establish preregistration; evaluator-controlled tool traces and artifact chronology substantiate evidence provenance; they do not receive separate process points. Report actual resources and unknown measurements honestly, and retain failed branches.

`claims` link numerical evidence to an inference and scope; `validations` state what was checked and whether it supports, limits or invalidates a claim. Evidence requirements follow the claims: a stationary-point claim requires defensible stationary-point evidence, an energetic comparison compatible state/composition/reference definitions, a spectral assignment physical state correspondence, and a causal conclusion a design capable of testing it. No particular program, named control or predetermined outcome is mandatory.

`complete` is your claim of an adequately addressed question, subject to scientific review. `partial`, `bounded_failure` and `input_blocked` preserve useful work without asserting completion. Evidence-supported negative or unresolved conclusions earn only the independent scientific-result items they establish; they do not automatically earn full credit for a self-selected scope; missing work or convergence failure alone cannot establish non-identifiability. Failure/blocked submissions require evidence and concrete recovery/release conditions. Format acceptance never awards scientific points. Report uncertainty and the limits of identification; no universal numerical tolerance is supplied for uncalibrated extensions.

Scoring applies to independent scientific-result items on one 100-point scale. Each item earns its assigned points only when its specific scientific proposition is supported by valid evidence. Reporting an opposite, unresolved or incomplete result is allowed but does not automatically earn that item or full-question credit. Method freedom and honest reporting do not remove the result-specific scientific standard.

Final answers are scored under paper_science_v3 / final_results_100. Each required scientific result must be supported by its relevant calculations or analysis; structures, executions and research records are evidence, not independent score items. An established equivalent explanation passes; insufficient or scientifically invalid support fails the affected conclusion.

Research decision records may be an empty array, including for `complete` submissions. Their presence or number is not a scoring or completion requirement; conclusions still require the relevant scientific evidence.
