1. General information
   Chemicals used in synthesis, including dichloromethane (DCM), n-hexane, ethyl acetate (EA),
methanol (MeOH), tetrahydrofuran (THF), acetone and petroleum ether (PE), were purchased from
commercial sources and used without further purification unless indicated otherwise. Silica gel for
analytical thin layer chromatography (TLC) and column chromatography (200 ~ 300 mesh) was
purchased from Qingdao Haiyang Chemical Co., Ltd & Special Silica Gel Factory. NMR spectra
(1H NMR spectra at 400 MHz, 500 MHz or 600 MHz, 13C NMR spectra at 100 MHz, 125 MHz or
150 MHz, two-dimensional NMR spectra at 400, 500 or 600 MHz) were recorded on JEOL or
Bruker NMR spectrometer. High resolution MALDI-TOF or APCI mass spectrometric analyses
were carried out using a Bruker Autoflex speed or Agilent 1290-6545XT mass spectrometer. UV-
vis-NIR spectra were measured on a UV-3600i Plus spectrometer from Shimadzu. Cyclic
voltammetry (CV) and differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) studies were carried out on a CH
Instrument Model 660E electrochemical system utilizing a three-electrode configuration consisting
of a glassy carbon (working electrode), platinum wire (counter electrode) and SCE (saturated
calomel electrode, reference electrode) in a solution of 0.1 M TBAPF6 in dry DCM at a scan rate of
50 mV s–1 in a nitrogen-filled cell. EPR spectrum was carried out using CIQTEK EPR 200M ESR
spectrometer and instrument parameters: ν = 9.46 GHz, modulation frequency 100 kHz, modulation
amplitude 1.0 G, and microwave power 3.98 mW. Crystallographic data were collected using a
Rigaku Oxford Diffraction XtaLAB Synergy diffractometer equipped with a HyPix-6000E area
detector at 100 K using Cu Kα (λ = 1.54184 Å) from a PhotonJet micro-focus X-ray source or
Bruker D8 Venture Txs Photon Ildiffractometer equipped with a HyPix-6000E area detector at 193
K using Ga Kα (λ =1.34139 Å) from a PhotonJet micro-focus X-ray source.

5. Quantum chemical calculations


Geometry optimizations and electronic structure computations were performed using
the ORCA  program  package[4]  and  time-dependent  density  functional  theory
calculations were  carried out using  the Gaussian 16  program.[5] For geometry
optimizations, two density functionals, B3LYP and BP86, were tested with different
initial guesses: closed-shell singlet, open-shell singlet, and triplet for complex 4. The
def2-TZVP basis set was applied to the Ni metal center and its first coordination sphere
(Cl1, Cl2, N1, N2, C1, C2; see Figure S5), whereas def2-SVP was used for all other
atoms. The optimized bond lengths (Table S1) were compared with the experimental
crystal  data, revealing  that the BP86  closed-shell  structure  exhibits the  closest
agreement with the crystallographic bond lengths. Despite this, irrespective of the
functional employed, computations starting from the open-shell singlet guess were
invariably found to converge to the same closed-shell singlet solution and the triplet
state was predicted to lie 25.1 kcal/mol (BP86) higher in energy than the closed-shell
singlet. All these findings revealed that complex 4 indeed features a closed-shell singlet
electronic structure. In line with this view, the metric parameters of the closed-shell
singlet calculated by the two functionals are all in reasonable agreement with the
experiment (Table S1). To ensure that the optimized geometry was at a minimum, only
positive frequencies were obtained for all optimized geometries.
In TD-DFT calculations, solvation effects in toluene were accounted for by using the
solvation model based on density (SMD) at B3LYP/def2-SVP level for all compounds.
The nucleus independent chemical shits (NICS) values and the anisotropy of induced
current density (ACID) were obtained using gauge independent atomic orbital (GIAO)
and continuous set of gauge transformation (CSGT) method, respectively, at the
B3LYP/def2-SVP level of theory.
