  A. Experimental Section

General Considerations
All reactions and manipulations were performed under ambient conditions in air unless otherwise
noted. All commercially available materials were used as received (9,10-anthracenedicarboxylic
acid: Ambeed;  anthracene:  Eastman;  copper  (II)  nitrate  trihydrate:  Fluka;  pyridine,  4-
methylpyridine, and 4-methoxypyridine: Sigma-Aldrich; 4-trifluoromethylpyridine: Ambeed).
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) Spectroscopy: 1H NMR data were recorded on an Agilent
DD2 400 MHz spectrometer. 13C{1H} NMR data were recorded on an Agilent DD2 600 MHz
spectrometer. All resonances in the 1H and 13C NMR spectra are referenced to residual proteo
dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, δ 2.50 ppm).
Electrospray Ionization Mass Spectrometry (ESI-MS): ESI-MS was recorded on a Shimadzu
Scientific Instruments QToF 9030 LC-MS system, equipped with a Nexera LC-40D xs UHPLC,
consisting of a CBM-40 Lite system controller, a DGU-405 Degasser Unit, two LC-40D XS
UHPLC pumps, a SIL-40C XS autosampler and a Column Oven CTO-40S in the negative mode.
Electronic Absorption Spectroscopy (UV-Vis): UV-Vis spectra were recorded on a Agilient
Cary 60 UV-vis spectrophotometer at room temperature in THF.
Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA): TGA was performed on a TA Instruments TGA550
analyzer under a N2 atmosphere. 2-5 mg of sample was loaded onto a tared Pt pan for analysis and
the temperature was ramped from 25 °C to 650 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min. For isothermal
experiments, the temperature was ramped from 25 °C to 160 °C and held at 160 °C for 90 minutes.
Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC): DSC measurements were performed on a TA
Instruments DSC250 instrument. 2-5 mg of sample was loaded in a TA Instruments Tzero Pan and
sealed with a Tzero Lid using a press. Temperature was initially cooled to and held at –10 °C for
5 minutes, then ramped from –10 °C to 300 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min.
Diffuse Reflectance Spectroscopy (DRS): DRS spectra were recorded on a Shimadzu 3600 Plus
Spectrophotometer with an ISR-603 integrating sphere with a BaSO4 reference in the UV-visible
range (190-800 nm). Data was collected in diffuse reflectance mode and then inverted and
normalized as a representation of absorbance. Samples were prepared by combining 10 mg of
MOF and 80 mg of BaSO4 in a mortar and pestle and grinding into a well-incorporated mixture
before loading into a sample cell.
Attenuated Total Reflectance Fourier Infrared (ATR-FTIR) Spectroscopy: FTIR spectra
were collected on solid samples of compounds using a Shimadzu IRTracer-100 spectrometer.
Optical-Photothermal Infrared Spectroscopy (O-PTIR): All spectral data were collected
using a mIRage-LS optical photothermal IR microscope (Photothermal Spectroscopy
Corporation, Santa Barbara, CA) integrated with a MIRcat Mid-IR quantum cascade laser
(Daylight Solutions, San Diego, CA) with a four-module-pulsed tunable frequency range from
932 cm-1 to 2348 cm-1. The mosaic brightfield image of the pressed sample (Figure S21 was
collected using a 10X refractive objective (Nikon Model No. MRH00105), while the spectra and

crystal images (Figures S16-S20) were collected using a 40X Schwarzchild objective (PIKE
Model No. 891-0007) in standard mode. For each crystal imaged, spectra were collected at 3 or 4

Plasma Etching Silicon Wafers: Plasma etching of thermal oxide coated silicon wafers was
performed by subjecting wafers to O2 plasma for at least two minutes in a Plasma Etch, Inc. PE-
25LF etcher using ambient air as the O2 source.
Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM): SEM imaging was performed on a Hitachi SU8230 UHR
Cold Field Emission (CFE) SEM. Samples of pristine 1 and bulk annealed 2 were drop cast from
a methanol suspension onto 300 nm thermal oxide coated silicon wafers that had been plasma
etched for two minutes and allowed to air dry. Samples were sputter coated with gold (10 nm)
using a Leica ACE600 High Vacuum Sputter prior to analysis due to the insulating nature of the
MOF.
X-Ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS): XPS was collected on a PHI VersaProbe III AD
Scanning XPS Microprobe. Samples of pristine 1 and bulk annealed 2 were prepared by densely
packing material onto double-sided tape.
Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM): AFM images were collected using a Bruker Dimension
Fastscan AFM in peak force mode. Samples were prepared by growing crystals of 1 onto 300 nm
thermal oxide coated silicon wafers by adding the wafers to the MOF solvothermal synthesis and
subsequently washing the wafers with methanol and allowing to air dry. For AFM on annealed or
irradiated samples of 2Py, AFM was recorded in the pristine state (1) and again after annealing at
160 °C for 45 min or irradiation under N2 at 254 nm for 6 h. Images were processed in Gwyddion.
Single Crystals X-Ray Diffraction (SCXRD): Low-temperature diffraction data (ω-scans) were
collected on a Rigaku MicroMax-007HF diffractometer coupled to a Saturn994+ CCD detector
with Cu Kα (λ = 1.5418 Å) (1, 2DMF, 1OMe) or a Rigaku 007 HF+ diffractometer coupled to a
Pilatus3R 200KA detector with Mo Kα (λ = 0.7107 Å) (1Me). The diffraction images were
processed and scaled using Rigaku Oxford Diffraction software (CrysAlisPro; Rigaku OD: The
Woodlands, TX, 2015). Using Olex2.1 the structure was solved with the SHELXT2 structure
solution programing using Intrinsic Phasing and refined against F2 on all data by full-matrix least
squares with SHELXL. All non-hydrogen atoms were refined anisotropically. Hydrogen atoms
were included in the model at geometrically calculated positions and refined using a riding model.
The isotropic displacement parameters of all hydrogen atoms were fixed to 1.2 times the U value
of the atoms to which they are linked. The full numbering scheme of the compound 1 can be found
in the full details of the X-ray structure determination (CIF), which is included as Supporting
Information. The structure of 1, 2DMF, 1OMe, and 1Me have been deposited with the CCDC, with
deposition numbers 2486056, 2486055, 2486057, and 2486058, respectively. Data can be obtained
free    of   charge   from   The   Cambridge    Crystallographic   Data   Center    via
http://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/conts/retrieving.html

Powder X-Ray Diffraction (PXRD): Powder diffraction patterns were collected on a Rigaku
MiniFlex600 Powder X-ray Diffractometer with Cu Kα (λ = 1.5418 Å) radiation at room
temperature. PXRD of 1 after annealing at 160 °C for various timepoints (Figure SX) were

Photomasking: A Si wafer was coated with a layer of Apiezon type N grease, which was pressed
grease side down onto a sample of 1 to coat the wafer. Excess MOF was removed by gently tapping
the wafer on edge. The wafer was placed coating side up on a glass slide, and a laminated paper
photomask was applied to the MOF coating and secured with tape. The wafer was brought into a
N2 drybox and placed in a Pyrex dish. A fine layer of silicone grease was applied to the edge of
the dish and a quartz window was pressed onto the grease to seal the sample under N2. The dish
was removed from the drybox and irradiated with 254 nm light in a Fisher Scientific 175 W UV
crosslinker for 3 h. The sample was removed from the dish and the photomask was gently removed
to reveal the masked image.
Variable Temperature (VT) Diffuse Reflectance Spectroscopy: VT diffuse reflectance spectra

Vibrating Sample Magnetometry (VSM): Magnetometry data was recorded on a Quantum
Design PPMS Dynacool physical property measurement system with a VSM head and large bore

4 capsule and packed with glass wool. Magnetic moments were recorded between 2 and 300 K
and converted from emu to cm3Kmol-1. Diamagnetic corrections were calculated and applied using
Pascal’s Constants for all elements and functional groups found in the structures. Data fitting for
1 was performed using the PHI software package.1 Fitting for 2R (R= Py, 4-MePy, 4-OMePy, 4-
CF3Py) was performed using a variation of the Bleaney-Bowers equation.2, 3
Computational Methods:  Geometry optimization was performed using the ORCA program
6.0.14, 5 for triplet states using the B3LYP functional6 and def2-SVP basis set.7 The calculations

utilized the atom-pairwise dispersion correction with the Becke-Johnson damping scheme
(D3BJ),8 def2/J auxiliary basis functions9 for density fitting integrals, and COSX numerical
integration for HF exchange (the RIJCOSX keyword for ORCA program).10
For the calculations of singlet-triplet (S-T) gaps, the same functional and basis set were employed
with def2-svp-jkfit auxiliary basis function in PySCF. The multi-collinear SF-TDDFT11, 12 is
implemented and available in PySCF-forge.13, 14 The SF-TDDFT reference state is the spin-
unrestricted DFT calculations of Sz=1 component triplet states. The singlet and triplet states were
obtained by flipping spin-up to spin-down electrons. The S-T gap was obtained as the spin-flip
energy difference between singlet and triplet states. We note that for conventional collinear SF-
TDDFT, the spin-flip triplet and the reference triplet states are not degenerate. The more rigorous
multi-collinear SF-TDDFT (without Tamm-Dancoff Approximation, TDA) removes this non-
physical degeneracy breaking.
Additional details on benchmarking singlet-triplet energy gaps and calculating natural transition
orbitals can be found in Section J.

Synthetic Methods
Synthesis of anthracene/9,10-anthracenedicarboxylic acid heterodimer (diAn(COOH)2). A 125 mL
Pyrex  Erlenmeyer  flask was  charged  with  anthracene  (0.356  g,  2 mmol)  and  9,10-
anthracenedicarboxylic acid (0.500 g, 1.88 mmol), a stir bar, and 70 mL of tetrahydrofuran. The
solution was sealed with a rubber septum and electrical tape and sparged with N2 for 60 minutes.
The yellow solution was irradiated with 365 nm light while vigorously stirring for 24 h, at which
point the solution had turned colorless with a fine colorless precipitate. The solution was vacuum
filtered through a Buchner funnel and the precipitate was washed three times with 10 mL of THF.
The filtrate solvent was removed under vacuum to produce a beige, microcrystalline powder
identified as the THF solvate of diAn(COOH)2 (1.57 THF per dimer as determined by 1H NMR after
exposure to vacuum for 60 h).
Yield: 0.997 g, 1.79 mmol, 95 %

Synthesis of Cu2(pyridine)4(diAn(COO)2)2 (1) Method 1. A one dram vial was charged with
Cu(NO3)2(H2O)3 (0.0050 g, 0.0207 mmol), diAn(COOH)2 (0.010 g, 0.0225 mmol), 3 drops of
pyridine, and 2 mL DMF. The vial was sealed and heated to 100 °C in a Binder FD023 forced
convection oven for 16 h to produce clumps of blue elongated rhombus-shaped plate crystals
suitable for single crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD).
Synthesis of 1 Method 2. A 4 dram vial was charged with Cu(NO3)2(H2O)3 (0.020 g, 0.828 mmol),
diAn(COOH)2 (0.040 g, 0.900 mmol), 0.5 mL of pyridine, and 8 mL DMF. The vial was sealed and
heated to 85 °C for 16 h in a Binder FD023 forced convection oven to produce a pale blue

                                    S6

crystalline powder. The precipitate was centrifuged and washed once with 10 mL DMF, then twice
with 10 mL methanol, centrifuging and removing the supernatant after each wash, and finally left
to air dry. Bulk purity was confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD).
Synthesis of Cu2(4-methylpyridine)4(diAn(COO)2)2 (1Me) Method 1. A 1 dram vial was charged with
Cu(NO3)2(H2O)3 (0.0050 g, 0.0207 mmol), diAn(COOH)2 (0.010 g, 0.0225 mmol), 3 drops of 4-
methylpyridine, and 2 mL DMF. The vial was sealed and heated to 90 °C in a Binder FD023 forced
convection oven for 16 h to produce blue square plate crystals suitable for single crystal X-ray
diffraction (SCXRD). Bulk purity was confirmed by PXRD.
Synthesis of Cu2(4-methoxypyridine)4(diAn(COO)2)2 (1OMe) Method 1. A 1 dram vial was charged
with Cu(NO3)2(H2O)3 (0.0050 g, 0.0207 mmol), diAn(COOH)2 (0.010 g, 0.0225 mmol), 3 drops of
4-methoxypyridine, and 2 mL DMF. The vial was sealed and heated to 90 °C in a Binder FD023
forced convection oven for 16 h to produce blue square plate crystals suitable for single crystal X-
ray diffraction (SCXRD). Bulk purity was confirmed by PXRD.
Synthesis of Cu2(DMF)2(An(COO)2)4 (2DMF). A 1 dram vial was charged with Cu(NO3)2(H2O)3
(0.0050 g, 0.0207 mmol), diAn(COOH)2 (0.010 g, 0.0225 mmol), 15 drops of pyridine, and 4 mL
DMF. The vial was sealed and heated to 120 °C in a Binder FD023 forced convection oven for 16
h to produce orange/brown square block crystals suitable for X-ray diffraction. Bulk purity was
confirmed by PXRD.
General method for the synthesis of retrocyclized species Cu2(4-RPy)2(An(COO)2)4 (R= H, Me,
OMe) (2Py, 2Me, 2OMe) by thermal annealing. A sample of pristine MOF in a vial was placed in a
Quincy Lab model 10GC oven set to 160 °C until sublimated anthracene crystals were no longer
visible on the sides of the vial (45-60 min). The sample was removed from the oven and allowed
to cool to room temperature. Speciation was confirmed by PXRD.
Synthesis of 2Py by UV light irradiation. In a N2 drybox, a quartz cuvette was charged with a
powder sample of 1. The cuvette threads were sealed with Teflon tape and the cap was sealed
externally with electrical tape. The cuvette was irradiated with 254 nm light in a Fisher Scientific
175 W UV crosslinker for 24 h, shaking the powder sample every 1-3 h to expose pristine material.
The sample was analyzed by PXRD, IR, and DRS.

   J. Theory Section
Benchmark calculations of single-triplet energy gaps
A comparison between calculated and experimentally measured singlet-triplet gaps is given in
Table S4. The calculated values agree well with the experimental measurement, both in absolute
value and the relative trend. To assess the accuracy of calculated S-T gaps, we performed
benchmark calculations for the functional dependence, basis-set effects, and the dependence on
equilibrium structures. All the calculations in this subsection were performed for the pyridine
(R=H) variant. The calculated S-T gaps using SF-TDA with B3LYP, PBE015, and CAM-B3LYP16
functionals are 335, 316, and 308 cm-1, respectively. The three hybrid functionals reasonably agree
with each other. However, SF-TDA with PBE functional17 overestimate the S-T gap to 609 cm-1,
demonstrating the importance of including exact Hartree-Fock exchange. The SF-TDDFT with
B3LYP functional gives a similar prediction on S-T gap as 344 cm-1 to that from SF-TDA, the
difference between which is negligible in this context. To test the basis-set convergence, we used
def2-TZVP basis set for Cu atoms. The calculated S-T gap with triple-zeta basis set is 343 cm-1,
showing negligible basis-set effects. We further optimized the equilibrium structure using minimal

calculations with ORCA. The S-T gap at CASSCF (2,2) geometry is 340 cm-1, which again is very
close to that obtained at DFT geometry.
