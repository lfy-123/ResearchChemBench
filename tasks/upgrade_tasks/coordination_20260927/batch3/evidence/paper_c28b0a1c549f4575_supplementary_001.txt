Experimental Section/Methods

1. Materials

    The bromoethane was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd.

(Shanghai, China). The triethylenediamine and fluoboric acid (HBF4, 50% aqueous

solution) were purchased from Tianjin Guangfu Fine Chemical Research Institute

(Tianjin, China). The TEA-BF4 (> 99%) and PC solvent (battery grade, extra dry < 20

ppm of water) was purchased from Jiangsu Guotai Super Power New Material Co.

Ltd. (Jiangsu, China).

2. Electrolyte synthesis/purification

    The DEDABCO-BF4 was synthesized via a two-step method.

     Step 1: Triethylenediamine (11.22 g, 0.1 mol) was dissolved in anhydrous

ethanol and poured into a three-necked flask. Then, bromoethane (27.24 g, 0.25 mol)

was added to a beaker containing an appropriate amount of absolute ethanol. The

mixed solution of bromoethane and absolute ethanol was added to the constant

pressure dropping funnel and dropped into the three-necked flask. After bromoethane

was added dropwise,  the  reaction was  further  stirred  at 78℃for 5 h  in  the

atmospheric  atmosphere.  The  solvent  was  cooled  to room  temperature  and

precipitated the crystals. The solution was suction filtered, and the filter cake was

washed with absolute ethanol to obtain a crude product. The crude product was

dissolved in anhydrous ethanol, then heated to clarify boiling, and then cooled to

crystallization, finally filtered by suction. The purified intermediate product N, N-1,

4-diethyl-trivinyl diammonium bromide (DEDABCO-Br) was obtained by repeating

=== PDF PAGE 3 ===
the above steps 3 times. DEDABCO-Br was obtained (25.08 g, yield: 75.98%) after

drying the white solid powder in a vacuum oven at 60℃till constant weight.

     Step 2: DEDABCO-Br (33.01 g, 0.1 mol) and HBF4 (35.13 g, 0.2 mol) were

dissolved in 250 mL of deionized water and stir at room temperature for 30 minutes to

ensure complete  reaction. Subsequently, we  dissolved  the  solid crude product

obtained through the rotary evaporation method back into deionized water. After

re-crystallization with anhydrous ethanol multiple times,  it will be further purified

using activated carbon and Al2O3. The DEDABCO-BF4 was obtained (28.96 g, yield:

84.21%) after drying for 48 h at 80°C under a vacuum and then stored in a sealed

container in a glove box with high pure argon (< 1 ppm O2 and < 1 ppm H2O). The

schematic diagram of its preparation process is shown in Figure S1. Finally, we used

nuclear magnetic hydrogen spectroscopy to determine the purity of the obtained

product. Figure S2 shows that the obtained product is DEDABCO-(BF4)2.

    At room temperature and glove box environment, TEA-BF4 was dissolved in PC

with the concentration of 1 mol kg-1 (1M). We added 0.1M, 0.2M, and 0.3M

DEDABCO-(BF4)2 respectively to the 1M TEA-BF4/PC electrolyte, and labeled them

as  1+0.1,  1+0.2,  and  1+0.3. We  also  used 1M  TEA-BF4/PC  and  0.3M

DEDABCO-(BF4)2/PC as control groups, labeled as 1M TEA and 0.3M DED (the

solubility of DEDABCO-(BF4)2 in PC is the highest at 0.3M). Adding an appropriate

amount  of 4Å  molecular  sieve, and  using  the  Karl  Fischer  Moisture  Tester

(Mettler-Toledo C20, Switzerland) to measure the moisture to be less than 20 ppm

after standing for a week before use.

3. Electrolyte synthesis/purification
    The negative electrode materials were mixed to a viscous block using 85 wt%

mesoporous carbon (MC), 7 wt% carbon black (VXC72) as the conductor, and 8 wt%

poly(tetrafluoroethylene) (PTFE) as the binder. Meanwile, the positive electrode were

also stirred to a sticky state with mixing 82 wt% microporous activated carbon (AC)

(Kuraray, YP-50), 10 wt% carbon black (VXC72) as the conductor, and 8 wt% PTFE

as the binder. Subsequently, they were individually rolled into films and pressed onto

a carbon-coated aluminum foil to obtain the corresponding electrodes. The electrodes

were punched into wafers with the diameter of 13 mm and therewith dried under

vacuum at 120 ℃for over 12 hours before usage, achieving active material mass

loadings of around 4.5 mg cm-2. Active material loading on positive and negative

were the same [1].

3. Preparation of Electrode and Supercapacitors

    The structure and purity of DEDABCO-BF4 samples were characterized by

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) (Bruker Avance 500 spectrometer, Switzerland).

A conductivity meter (Mettler-Toledo S30, Switzerland) was used to determine the

relationship between the conductivity and concentration of the  electrolyte. The

viscosity of the electrolyte is tested by a viscosity testing device (A&D SV-10, Japan).

The structure information of the electrolyte was obtained by Raman spectroscopy

(Horiba, Japan). Under the three-electrode configuration, the electrochemical stability

window (ESW)  of  the  electrolyte  was  evaluated  using  the  linear  scanning

voltammetry method (Autolab PGSTAT302N, Switzerland). The three electrodes

consisted of a titanium working electrode, a clean platinum counter electrode, and an

=== PDF PAGE 5 ===
Ag/AgNO3 reference electrode. Two ESW tests were conducted at a scan rate of 5

mV/s: one from 0 to 5 V and the other from 0 to -5 V.

   AC//MC asymmetrical supercapators (ASCs) coin cells were prepared in an

argon-filled  glove box  for  electrochemical measurements  of  electrolytes. The

galvanostatic charge/discharge tests (GCD) and cycling performance were tested at

the Arbin battery test system (BT2000, America) and the Land battery test system

(CT2001A, China). The cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance

spectroscopy (EIS) were tested using Autolab (PGSTAT302N, Switzerland) and an

electrochemical workstation (CHI660E, China). The gravity specific capacitance of

5. Computational details

   We performed  structural optimizations for the anions,  cations, and solvent

molecules using the Gaussian 16 software with density functional theory (DFT)

=== PDF PAGE 6 ===
adopted the B3LYP functional and the D3BJ dispersion correction [2-4]. Geometry

optimization and frequency calculations were performed for  all atoms using the

def2-SVP basis set [5]. Based on these optimized structures, the binding energy

between ions and solvent molecules was calculated at the B3LYP/6-311+G(2d,p)

level, while the adsorption energies on the electrode surface were calculated at the

B3LYP-D3BJ/def2-TZVP level. Additionally, the highest occupied molecular orbital

(HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels were

determined directly from the B3LYP/6-311G** optimized structures.

    Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed in GROMACS[6] to

investigate electrolyte solvation structures. Initial systems were constructed using

Packmol [7], populating simulation boxes with electrolyte ions, solvent molecules,

and diluents according to Table S1. The General Amber Force Field (GAFF) is used

to describe TEA+, DED2+, BF4- and PC [8,9], the corresponding atomic partial charges

were derived using the Restrained Electrostatic Potential (RESP) fitting procedure

[10]. Energy minimization was first conducted under periodic boundary conditions

(PBC) using the conjugate gradient algorithm (step size = 0.01 nm). Non-bonded

interactions employed the Verlet cutoff scheme with a 1.2 nm cutoff for both van der

Waals and electrostatic interactions; the latter were calculated using the Particle Mesh

Ewald (PME) method [11]. In the reciprocal space, an Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)

grid spacing of 0.1 nm and cubic interpolation are used to calculate the electrostatic

interactions. The solution of the dynamic equation employs a leap integrator with a

step size of 2 nm.

=== PDF PAGE 7 ===
    The entire simulation was performed in the NPT ensemble. Temperature was

maintained at 298.15 K using the Nose-Hoover thermostat with a coupling constant of

1.0  ps,  while  pressure was  controlled  at  1  bar  via  the  Parrinello-Rahman

barostat (coupling constant: 1.0 ps) [12]. The balance process is as follows: The

system was gradually heated from 0 K to 298.15 K under NPT conditions using a

single-step  annealing  protocol.  Temperature  coupling was  achieved by  using

the V-rescale thermostat and isotropic pressure was controlled via the C-rescale

algorithm. In the second balance stage, the system continues to operate in the NPT

ensemble for an additional 30 ns, with a time step of 0.001 ps. The temperature is

maintained at 298.15 K through the V-rescaled thermodynamic mechanism, while the

pressure is controlled by the Parrinello-Rahman pressure statistical mechanism. For

production runs, the system simulates 10 nanoseconds in the NPT ensemble, with a

time step of 0.001 picoseconds. After completing the molecular dynamics simulation,

the  radial  distribution  functions (RDF)  of  the "TEA+-PC"  and "DED2+-PC"

interactions and the average square displacement (MSD) of the PC molecules were

analyzed. The most likely solvation structures in each electrolyte were determined and

quantified. The simulation box of the electrolyte was visualized using VMD.
