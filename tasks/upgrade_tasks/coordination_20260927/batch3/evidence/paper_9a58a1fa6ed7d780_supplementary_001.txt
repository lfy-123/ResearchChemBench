1 General Information

Methods: 1H NMR, 13C NMR and 11B NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker ASCENDTM operating

at 400 MHz MHz for 1H NMR, 101 MHz for 13C NMR, and 128 MHz for 11B NMR. The residual solvent

signals were used as references for 1H and 13C NMR spectra and the chemical shifts converted to the

TMS scale (CDCl3: δH = 7.26 ppm, δC = 77.16 ppm; CD2Cl2: δH = 5.32 ppm, δC = 53.84 ppm;

(CD3)2SO: δH = 2.50 ppm, δC = 39.52 ppm). Multiplicitys were reported as follows (s = singlet, d =

doublet, t = triplet, q = quartet, m = multiplet, and br = broad), coupling constant (J values) in Hz. High

resolution mass spectral (HRMS) data were acquired on waters G2-S Qtof spectrometer (electrospray

ionization: ESI). Data for the single-crystal structure determinations were collected with Rigaku XtalAB

PRO MM007 DW diffractometer (for compounds 3a, 3e and 3i using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54184 Å),

3j using Mo Kα (λ = 0.71073)) or Rigaku HyPix-Arc 150 diffractometer (for compounds 3b, 5a and 5b

using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54184 Å)) Software packages used: Olex 2 for structure solution and

refinement, Mercury and Diamond for graphics. The absorption spectra of all compounds were measured

by SPECORD® 210 PLUS spectrometers. Fluorescence measurements were carried out with an

Edinburgh Instruments FS5 fluorescence spectrometer. Solution quantum yields were collected with a

calibrated integrating sphere system. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves were performed with a CHI660E

potentiostat. Thermogravimetric analysis was performed using a METTLER TOLEDO Thermal

Analysis Instrument. Samples were loaded into alumina crucible and heated at a constant rate of

10 °C/min under a nitrogen purge (10 mL/min). Theoretical calculations were performed using the

Gaussian 16 software and Multiwfn. The geometries were optimized as the B3LYP/6-311G (d, p) level,

and energies were calculated at the same level of theory. Nucleus independent chemical shifts (NICS),

LOL-π and ICSS plots were calculated using the gauge invariant atomic orbital (GIAO) approach at the

B3LYP/6-311G (d, p) level. Hirshfeld surface analysis was calculated by Multiwfn and redered via

VMD. VMD is developed with NIH support by the Theoretical and Computational Biophysics group at

the Beckman Institute, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.

Materials: BN-Naphthanene was synthesized by the reported method.1 Some solvents used in the

experiment needed  to be  dried and deoxygenated,  including  tetrahydrofuran, dichloromethane,

triethylamine and toluene. They were dried with calcium hydride and distilled atmospheric pressure.

4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolane  and   2-isopropoxy-4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolane

were dried under sodium and distilled under reduced pressure. The commercially available triethylamine

and diallylamine need to be re-distilled and deoxygenated. Other commercially available chemicals were

used without further purification unless otherwise noted. All yields given referred to isolated yields.

2 Synthesis and Characterisation

2.1   General Procedure





                        Scheme S1. Synthesis of aryl boronic acid ester.

General procedure A for aryl boronic acid ester: Zinc powder (1.3 g, 20 mmol, 3.5 eq.) and Ni(dppf)Cl2

([1,1'-bis(diphenylphosphino)ferrocene]dichloronickel (II), 339.0 mg, 0.5 mmol, 8 mol%), was added

to the flask and replaced with argon three times. Toluene (10 mL), o-dibromoarene (0.7 mL, 5.8 mmol,

1.0 eq.), and triethylamine (4.3 mL, 30 mmol, 5.2 eq.) were added. Then 4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-

dioxaborolane (2.9 mL, 20 mmol, 3.5 eq.) was added dropwise via syringe and the resulting solution

was stirred at 100 °C in an oil bath for 24 h. After the reaction was quenched with NH4Cl (aq.), the

product was extracted with ethyl acetate. The combined organic layers were dried over Na2SO4, and

concentrated under reduced pressure. Purification of the residue by column chromatography on C–300

silica gel (petroleum ether/dichloromethane = 2/1) afforded the crude products. The crude products were

further purified by preparative HPLC or recrystallization to afford products as white solids or colorless

oil.1





                      Scheme S2. Synthesis of BN-fluoranthene derivatives.

General procedure B for boron-nitrogen doped fluoranthene derivatives: Aryl boronic acid ester

(0.74 mmol, 1.05 eq.), 1,8-dibromo-9,10-B,N-naphthalene (1a, 200.0 mg, 0.70 mmol, 1.0 eq.), Pd2dba3

(64.1 mg, 0.07 mmol, 10 mol%), tris(2-Methoxyphenyl)phosphine (98.7 mg, 0.28 mmol, 40 mol%),

K2CO3 (580.5 mg, 4.20 mmol, 6.0 eq.) was added to flask, THF (25 mL) and water (2.5 mL) was added

to the mixture under Ar. The solution was heated to 85 ℃ in an oil bath for 24 h. After cooled to room

temperature, the aqueous layer was extracted with ethyl acetate (50 mL) for three times. The combined

organic layers were dried over Na2SO4, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The residue was

purified by flash column chromatography (petroleum ether/dichloromethane = 5/1 to 3/1) to obtain

yellow or orange solid products.

                 Scheme S3. Synthesis of BN- or CC-acenaphto[1,2-k]fluoranthene.

General procedure C for boron-nitrogen fluoranthene derivatives: Aryl boronic acid ester (6b, 81.0

mg, 0.14 mmol, 0.5 eq.), 1,8-dibromo-9,10-B, N-naphthalene (1a, 80.0 mg, 0.28 mmol, 1.0 eq.) or 1,8-

mol%), K2CO3 (464.4 mg, 3.36 mmol, 12.0 eq.) was added to a sealing tube, toluene (2.0 mL) and water

(0.2 mL) was added to the mixture in the glove box. The solution was heated to 130 °C in an oil bath

for 24 h. After cooling to room temperature, Na2SO4 was added to dry the mixture. The organic matter

was extracted with dichloromethane (50 mL) and filtered to remove insoluble impurities. Solvent was

removed by concentration under reduced pressure, anhydrous methanol (2 ml) was added to sonicate for

2 min then filtered. The solid residue was collected and washed twice with anhydrous methanol to obtain

as red solid (5a) or yellow solid (5b).

2.3   Synthetic Details and Characterisation of BN-CPPAHs

1,8-Dibromo-9,10-B,N-naphthalene (1a)





                   Scheme S4. Synthesis of 1,8-dibromo-BN-naphthalene (1a).

Under argon atmosphere, BN-naphthalene (2.0 g, 15.5 mmol) was dissolved in 50 ml CH2Cl2, and cooled

to −35 °C. AlBr3 (8.68 g, 32.5 mmol) and N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) (5.79 g, 32.5 mmol) were added

to another flask, cooled in an ethanol liquid nitrogen bath, and cold dichloromethane (300 mL) was

carefully added dropwise to the above flask. The system was slowly heated to 25 °C and then stirred 30

min. The resulting suspension was transferred to a CH2Cl2 solution of 1a and stirred at –35 °C for 2

hours, then heated to 25 °C for additional 2 hours. After the reaction was completed by monitoring by

TLC, the insoluble matter was removed by filtration. The filtrate was further concentrated to dryness.

The resulting residue was purified by short column chromatography (elute: hexane) to obtain 2.49 g

(56.0% yield) of 1a as a white solid.

Rf = 0.22 (petroleum ether). 1H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl3, 298 K, ppm): δ = 8.05 (d, J = 7.28 Hz, 2H;

Ar-H), 7.78 (d, J = 6.92 Hz, 2H; Ar-H), 6.59 (t, J = 6.1, 6 Hz, 2H). The 1H NMR spectroscopic data is

accordance with literature report.2

                   Scheme S5. Synthesis of 1,3,8-tribromo-B,N-naphthalene (1b)

then 25 ℃ for 2 hours. After the reaction was completed by TLC monitoring, the insoluble suspension

was removed by filtration. The filtrate was further concentrated to dryness. The resulting residue was

purified by flash column chromatography on silica gel (eluting with petroleum ether/CH2Cl2 = 5/1) to

obtain 1b as white solid (176.4 mg, 62.2% yield).

7 Device Fabrication and Characterization

  SiO2/Si wafer (SiO2 300 nm thick, 10 nFcm−2) was first washed by deionized water deionized water,

isopropyl alcohol and dried by a N2 flow. Then the wafer was cleaned by O2 plasma, followed by the

octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS) modification. OTS was thermally evaporated onto wafer at 200 °C for 2

h in vacuum. Then the SiO2/Si wafer was washed by n-hexane, trichloromethane, and isopropyl alcohol

and finally dried by N2 flow. Crystals were grown directly onto the OTS modified SiO2/Si wafer by

microspacing in-air sublimation. Gold was used as the source and drain contacts by the stamping

electrodes method. All the electrical measurements were conducted in the air at room temperature with

a Keithley 4200-SCS connected a probe station. The mobility was extracted from the saturation region

using the equation of IDS = Ciμ(W/2L)(VG − VT). IDS is the drain current, W (85 μm) and L (30 μm) are

the channel width and length, respectively. Ci is the capacitance of the insulation layer, and VG and VT

are the gate voltage and threshold voltage, respectively.
