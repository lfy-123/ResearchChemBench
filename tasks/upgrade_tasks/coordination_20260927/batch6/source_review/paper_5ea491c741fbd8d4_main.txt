A B S T R A C T
In this study, a novel isoxazole derivative, (E)-4-(4-methoxybenzylidene)-3-methylisoxazol-5(4H)-one (1a) was 
synthesized and characterized using various spectroscopic analysis methods (1H-NMR, 13C-NMR). Also, the 
compound 1a was characterized by single-crystal X-ray diffraction, crystallizing in the monoclinic space group 
P21/c with a= 12.8218(8) Å, b = 6.9144(5) Å, c= 12.7665(8) Å, α= 90◦, β= 111.969(2)◦and γ= 90◦. The 
structures of 1a, showing that the molecules are associated by C—H⋅⋅⋅O interactions forming a supramolecular 
network. To understand the molecular basis of this activity, a multi-level density functional theory approach was 
employed. Compound 1a demonstrated the lowest HOMO-LUMO energy gap (3.6191 eV in gas phase and 3.5454 
eV in solvent phase), high electronic softness, indicative of efficient charge transfer and reactivity. Additional 
analyses, including global reactivity descriptors, electrostatic surface potential, and intramolecular interactions 
that support biological activity. Furthermore, the molecule 1a exhibits a significant affinity with the CDK2 
protein and a low binding energy of -7.8 kcal/mol, according to the findings of the in silico molecular docking 
investigation. Therefore, it could be a potential small molecule that looks like a drug to treat proliferative ill­
nesses like cancer.
1. Introduction
Isoxazole derivatives are five-membered heterocyclic compounds 
containing three carbon atoms, one nitrogen atom, and one oxygen atom 
positioned adjacent to each other, forming an aromatic ring with the 
molecular formula C₃H₃NO. The aromatic nature of the isoxazole ring 
provides high chemical stability and electron delocalization, making it 
an important structural motif in medicinal and synthetic organic 
chemistry [1–3]. Isoxazole derivatives have attracted considerable 
attention due to their broad spectrum of pharmacological activities, 
including antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, anticancer, anti­
convulsant, antitubercular, and antiviral properties. Several clinically 
used drugs, such as leflunomide, incorporate the isoxazole nucleus as a 
key pharmacophore responsible for biological activity [4,5]. These 
compounds are also useful in drug design as bioisosteres for amide or 
phenolic groups and serve as valuable intermediates in organic synthesis 
and agrochemical development. Due to their structural versatility and 
biological significance, isoxazole derivatives continue to be extensively 
explored in modern drug discovery and development [6–8].
Isoxazole derivatives have emerged as promising inhibitors of human 
cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2), a crucial enzyme that regulates cell 
cycle progression from the G1 to S phase through phosphorylation of key 
substrates. Aberrant activation or over expression of CDK2 is often 
associated with uncontrolled cell proliferation and the development of 
various cancers, making it an attractive target for anticancer drug design 
[9–12]. The isoxazole nucleus provides an ideal scaffold for designing

2.4. DFT calculations
In the density functional theory (DFT) study of these wave functions, 
the structure was optimized using the 6-311+G(d,p) basis set and the 
B3LYP hybrid functional. The hybrid B3LYP functional was chosen due 
to its extensive validation for organic and heteroaromatic systems, 
providing an optimal compromise between computational cost and ac­
curacy. Prior reports have shown that B3LYP reliably predicts structural 
parameters, vibrational spectra, and electronic properties of isoxazole 
derivatives and related bioactive compounds, with results that closely 
match experimental observations. The 6-311+G(d,p) basis set was 
applied because it is a polarized split-valence basis incorporating diffuse 
functions (+) to better describe anionic species and delocalized elec­
trons, along with polarization functions (d on heavy atoms and p on 
hydrogen atoms) to enhance the accuracy of bond angles, electronic 
delocalization, and hydrogen-bonding interactions [23]. Consequently, 
the B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) level of theory is widely regarded as a standard 
approach for small to medium-sized organic molecules, offering a reli­
able and computationally efficient description of their electronic struc­
ture. The study of the frontier molecular orbital (FMO), its energy gap, 
and the dependent global and local indices were calculated using 
Koopman's approximation [24,25]. The molecular electrostatic poten­
tial (MEP) map is a very useful descriptor for determining the positions 
of electrophilic and nucleophilic regions. The MEP color-codes the 
electrostatic potential zones from red to dark blue (decrease in potential; 
blue >green >yellow >orange >red), indicating very negative and 
positive locations. The molecule's possible reactive sites were examined 
using the molecular electrostatic potential map. All calculations were 
performed using Gaussian 09 [26], and the results were shown using 
GaussView 5.0 [27].

3.1. Synthesis
The structure of the synthesized compound is confirmed as (E)-4-(4-

shift) value of sharp singlet at 2.29 ppm integrated for three protons, 
assigned to the methyl group at the C3 position of the isoxazolone ring. 
The δ value of methoxy protons (-OCH3) appeared as a three-proton

7.03-7.00 ppm, assigned to the aromatic protons ortho to the (-OCH3)

The 13C-NMR spectrum (100 MHz, CDCl3) showed 12 signals, in

at δ 168.7 ppm is assigned to the lactone carbonyl carbon. The δ value of 
C-3 carbon of the isoxazolone ring is 164.6 ppm, the δ value of the para-

3.2. X-ray crystallography
The molecular structure of 1a is shown in Fig. 1 (ORTEP view of 1a). 
The compound 1a is crystallized in the monoclinic crystal system with 
the space group P21/c. In the molecule, the 3-methylisoxazol-5(4H)-one 
ring and methoxyphenyl rings are bridged by CH group, is nearly planar 
with rmsd of 0.040(1) Å. Structural refinement was performed using 
2602 independent reflections selected from a total of 24592 measured 
reflections. All non-hydrogen atoms were refined anisotropically using 
full-matrix least-squares minimization. The refinement converged with 
an R-value of 4.50% and a goodness-of-fit (GOF) of 1.084.
The molecular configuration of 1a is stabilized by both intra and 
intermolecular interactions. The C6-H6...O3 intramolecular interactions 
are involved in the formation of S(7) self motifs. In the crystal, the 
molecules are linked by two major interactions of the type C-H)⋅⋅⋅O. The 
C8-H8...O3 and C12-H12B⋅⋅⋅O2 intermolecular interactions resulting in 
the formation of the R2
2(9) ring motifs in the crystal (Fig. 2). In addition 
to these interactions, π⋅⋅⋅π stacking interactions also contributed signif­
icantly to the overall stability of the 1a crystal structure. A π⋅⋅⋅π inter­
action is observed between the isoxazole ring (Cg(1): C9/C10/N1/O2/ 
C11) and the six-membered ring within the same moiety (Cg(2): C2/C3/

the π⋅⋅⋅π interaction.
3.3. Hirshfeld surface and fingerprint analysis
To quantitatively evaluate the intermolecular interactions present in 
the compound, Hirshfeld surface analysis along with two-dimensional 
(2D) fingerprint plots were utilized. These 2D plots help in visualizing 
and analyzing the nature of intermolecular contacts within the crystal 
structure, making it possible to identify distinct types of interactions and 
compare different crystal forms [34]. This approach offers meaningful 
insights into the compound’s molecular packing and structural stability. 
For the 1a, Hirshfeld surfaces were mapped over dnorm within the range 
of –0.676 to 1.415 Å to highlight close intermolecular contacts. The dark 
reds spots on the dnorm surface (Fig. 3(a)) are the characteristics of atoms 
participating in strong hydrogen bonds, thereby, confirming the pres­
ence of dimeric pairs of C12-H12B⋅⋅⋅O2 and C8-H8...O3 hydrogen bonds 
in the crystal. This is further confirmed by the electrostatic potential 
surfaces (as discussed in the later section). The weak intermolecular 
interactions in the 1a molecule were quantitatively analyzed using 
two-dimensional (2D) fingerprint plots (FPs), which illustrate the dis­
tribution of internal (di) and external (de) atomic distances. As depicted 
in Fig. 3(b), these plots offer a detailed representation of how various 
contacts contribute to the Hirshfeld surface.
The FPs (of overall contacts and individual atom…atom contacts) of 
FL are shown in Fig. 4. All the FPs are symmetric about the di = de line 
which is due to the homomeric nature of the interactions in the crystal. 
The highest contribution to the Hirshfeld surface is from H…H disper­
sions contributing 38.7%, O⋅⋅⋅H/H⋅⋅⋅O and C⋅⋅⋅C contacts contribute 
respectively 29.1% and 13.4% which can be attributed to the presence of 
C12-H12B⋅⋅⋅O2 interactions. The contributions of 9.7% and 6.2% by 
N⋅⋅⋅H/H⋅⋅⋅N and C⋅⋅⋅H/H...C contacts are because of π⋅⋅⋅π interactions 
operating in the crystal.
Shape index and curvedness curves help in identifying π…π stacking 
interactions. The π…π stacking interactions in the shape index appear as 
alternating red and blue triangles above the rings participating in the 
stacking, while they are characterized by a flat surface in the curvedness 
plot above the participating rings. Both these characteristics are 
observed in the shape index and curvedness plots (Fig. 5(a) and (b)) of 
the molecule and therefore gives evidence for π…π stacking interactions 
in the crystal.

marily cyclin E and cyclin A. Since CDK2 overexpression or hyper­
activation has been seen in a variety of malignancies, including ovarian, 
colorectal, lung, and breast cancers, it has therapeutic importance. 
Using tiny compounds to target CDK2 offers a way to specifically stop 
the growth of tumor cells while also perhaps making cancer cells more 
sensitive to radiation or chemotherapy. Furthermore, inhibiting CDK2 is 
important for overcoming treatment resistance since it is also linked to 
resistance mechanisms against currently available anticancer medicines 
[39].
Therefore, employing the structure 1B38 to analyze small-molecule 
inhibition aids in rational drug design, allowing the discovery of 
strong and specific CDK2 inhibitors with potential use in cancer and 
other proliferative illnesses. These complexes can be broken by small- 
molecule inhibitors that target CDK2, which stops DNA replication 
and cell cycle advancement.

4. Conclusions
This investigation integrates experimental, quantum chemical, and 
computational approaches to assess the anti-cancer potential of a novel a 
novel isoxazole derivative, (E)-4-(4-methoxybenzylidene)-3-methylisox­
azol-5(4H)-one (1a). The crystallographic analysis revealed that 1a 
crystallizes in monoclinic system with P21/c space group. The C8- 
H8⋅⋅⋅O3 and C12-H12B⋅⋅⋅O2 intermolecular interactions resulting in the 
formation of the R2
2(9) ring motifs in the crystal. Hirshfeld surface 
analysis indicated that van der Waals forces, particularly H⋅⋅⋅H in­
teractions (38.7 %), along with and O⋅⋅⋅H (29.1 %) N⋅⋅⋅H (9.7 %), play a 
significant role in maintaining the crystal’s stability. The HOMO-LUMO 
study indicated an energy gap of 3.6191 eV, signifying substantial 
chemical stability. The MEP analysis explored the reactive sites present 
in the molecule. Furthermore, the molecule 1a exhibits a significant 
affinity with the CDK2 protein and a low binding energy of -7.8 kcal/ 
mol, according to the findings of the in silico molecular docking inves­
tigation. Therefore, it could be a potential small molecule that looks like 
a drug to treat proliferative illnesses like cancer.
