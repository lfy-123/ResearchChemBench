Experimental Procedures 
Materials: 
1,4-Phenylenediboronic 
acid, 
2,5-dibromothiophene, 
2,5-dibromo-3-
thiophenecarboxylic acid, 3,4-thiophenedicarbonitrile and potassium carbonate was purchased from 
Bidepharm Ltd, Shanghai, China. Tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium was purchased from TCI 
Ltd. Thionyl chloride, tetrahydrofuran, 1,4-dioxane, LDA (2 M in solution in THF/n-
heptane/ethylbenzene), N,N-dimethylformamide and 1,2-dimethoxyethane was purchased from 
Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd. Phosphoric trichloride, ammonia solution, 
bromine, toluene, methanol and acetone was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Ltd. 
2,5-Dibromo-3-cyanothiophene1 and 2,5-dibromo-3,4-dicyanothiophene2 were prepared using 
previous literature methods. All chemicals were used as received and without further purification. 
 
Synthesis of L-Th-0CN 
 
In a typical reaction, a flask was charged with 1,4-phenylenediboronic acid (166 mg, 1 mmol), 2,5-
dibromothiophene (242 mg, 1 mmol), Pd(PPh3)4 (22.5 mg), an aqueous solution of K2CO3 (4.5 mL, 
2.0 M) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (25 mL). The mixture was degassed and filled with Ar over 3 
cycles and heated to 120 °C for 48 hours. The mixture was cooled to room temperature and poured 
into water. The precipitate was collected by filtration and washed with H2O, methanol, and acetone. 
Further purification of the polymers was carried out by Soxhlet extraction with THF for 48 h and 
the product was dried under a vacuum. The L-Th-0CN was obtained as yellow powders.

Synthesis of L-Th-2CN 
 
In a typical reaction, a flask was charged with 1,4-phenylenediboronic acid (166 mg, 1 mmol), 2,5-
dibromo-3,4-dicyanothiophene (292 mg, 1 mmol), Pd(PPh3)4 (22.5 mg), an aqueous solution of 
K2CO3 (4.5 mL, 2.0 M) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (25 mL). The mixture was degassed and filled 
with Ar over 3 cycles and heated to 120 °C for 48 hours. The mixture was cooled to room 
temperature and poured into water. The precipitate was collected by filtration and washed with H2O, 
methanol, and acetone. Further purification of the polymers was carried out by Soxhlet extraction 
with THF for 48 h and the product was dried under a vacuum. The L-Th-2CN was obtained as 
yellow powders. 
 
General methods: Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) measurements were performed on a Bruker 
D8 Advance diffractometer with Cu Kα1 radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS) data were obtained on Thermo ESCALAB250 instrument with a 
monochromatized Al Kα line source (200 W). The solid-state 13C NMR experiments were 
performed on a Bruker Avance III 500 spectrometer. The Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) and in-
suit FT-IR spectra were recorded in a wavelength ranging from 4000-500 cm-1 on a Thermo Nicolet 
IS50 spectrometer. Nitrogen adsorption/desorption isotherms were measured at 77 K with a 
Micromeritics ASAP 2460 analyzer. Before the measurements, the samples were degassed in 
vacuum at 393 K for 12 h. The Brunauer− Emmet−Teller (BET) method was utilized to calculate 


===== PDF PAGE 4 =====
 
 
4
the specific surface. The morphology of the samples was investigated using HITACHI SU8010 field 
emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images 
were obtained using a Thermo Fisher Scientific TEM (Tallos F200S). UV-Vis diffuse reflectance 
spectra (UV-Vis DRS) were performed on Varian Cary 500 Scan UV-visible system. Stead-state 
photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL spectroscopy were recorded at room temperature on 
a HORIBA Instruments FLS1000 spectrophotometer. Temperature-dependent PL spectra from 77 
to 300 K were also recorded on a HORIBA Instruments FLS1000 spectrophotometer. Electron 
paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra were obtained from a Bruker model A 300 spectrometer. 
Syeady-state surface photovoltage (SPV) signals were acquired with a CEL-SPS1000 spectrometer. 
Inductively coupled plasma mass spectra (ICP-MS) were conducted on a PerkinElmer Avio 200. 
Zeta potentials were measured on Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS90. Water contact angle was carried 
out by video optical contact angle tester (Dataphysics-OCA20). 
 
Photoelectrochemical measurements: The working electrode was prepared on indium-tin oxide 
(ITO) glass that was cleaned by sonication in ethanol for 30 min and dried at 353 K. The boundary 
of ITO glass was protected using Scotch tape. The 5 mg sample was dispersed in 900 μL DMF and 
100 μL Nafion by sonication to get a slurry. The slurry was spread onto pretreated ITO glass. After 
air-drying, the Scotch tape was unstuck, and the uncoated part of the electrode was isolated with 
epoxy resin. Mott-Schottky plots were achieved without any irradiation to obtain the flat-band 
potentials of the polymer films. 
The conversion of the Ag/AgCl reference potential to a normal hydrogen electrode (NHE) was 
calculated using the following equation: E(RHE) = E(Ag/AgCl) + Eϴ(Ag/AgCl) + 0.0591 × pH 
where Eϴ(Ag/AgCl) is 0.197 V. 
 
Photocatalytic H2 evolution test: Photocatalytic hydrogen evolution arrays were performed in a 
Pyrex top-irradiation reaction vessel linked to a glass closed gas system. H2 production was carried 


===== PDF PAGE 5 =====
 
 
5
out by dispersing 10 mg of photocatalyst powder in an aqueous solution (110 mL) containing 
triethanolamine (10 mL) as the sacrificial electron donor. 9.0 wt.% of Pt was loaded on the surface 
of the photocatalyst by the in-situ photodeposition approach using H2PtCl6. The mixture was 
evacuated several times to remove air completely before irradiation under a 300 W Xe-lamp. The 
wavelength of the incident light was controlled by using a long pass cut-off filter > 420 nm (or other 
appropriate long-pass cut-off filters) for testing the wavelength-dependence hydrogen evolution rate 
of the sample. The temperature of the reaction solution was kept at 12 °C by a flow of cooling water. 
The generated gases were analyzed by gas chromatography equipped with a thermal conductive 
detector (TCD) with Argon as the carrier gas. 
 
Photocatalytic O2 evolution test: Photocatalytic O2 production was measured in a Pyrex top-
irradiation reaction vessel connected to a glass closed gas circulation system. For each reaction, 10 
mg catalyst powder was well dispersed in an aqueous solution (100 mL) containing AgNO3 (0.01 
M) as an electron acceptor and La2O3 (0.2 g) as a pH buffer agent. The reactant solution was 
evacuated several times to completely remove air prior to irradiation under a 300 W Xe lamp and a 
water-cooling filter. The temperature of the reactant solution was maintained at 12 °C by a flow of 
cooling water during the reaction. The evolved gases were analyzed by gas chromatography 
equipped with a thermal conductive detector (TCD). 
 
Photocatalytic H2O2 production test: The photocatalytic reactions of hydrogen peroxide evolution 
were carried out in a Schlenk flask. In the Schlenk flask, 10 mg of catalyst powder was dispersed in 
20 mL deionized water by ultrasonication for 15 min. Then, O2 was bubbled through the solution 
for 15 min. Flowing water was employed to control the reaction temperature at 298 K. Then, the 
system was irradiated with a 50 W LED lamp (λ = 420 nm) After the reaction, the H2O2 amount was 
determined using a Shimadzu UV-1780 UV-Vis spectrophotometer by measuring its absorbance on 
the basis of the formation of the yellow complex TiIV-H2O2. When titanium oxysulfate (TiOSO4) 
reacted with H2O2, a yellow complex (pertitanic acid) was formed and UV−Vis measurement was 


===== PDF PAGE 6 =====
 
 
6
done at 410 nm to colorimetrically determine the concentration of H2O2 (TiO2+ +H2O2 → 
[TiO(H2O2)]2+). 
 
The apparent quantum yield (AQY) measurement for H2 evolution: The apparent quantum yield 
(AQY) for the reaction system was measured using monochromatic LED lamps with bandpass 
filters of 380, 420, 450, 500 and 600 nm, the intensities were 15.9, 16.5, 33.8, 14.6 and 7.2 mW cm-
1, respectively (Newport 2936-R optical power meter). The irradiation area was controlled as 3  3 
cm2. Depending on the amount of H2 produced by the photocatalytic reaction in one hour, the AQY 
was calculated according to the following equation: 
AQY (100%) = (2M*NA*h*c)/(S*P*t*λ) *100% 
where M is the amount of H2 molecules (mol), NA is Avogadro constant (6.022 × 1023 mol-1), h is 
the Planck constant (6.626 × 10-34 J·s), c is the speed of light (3.0 × 108 m s-1), S is the irradiation 
area (cm2), P is the intensity of irradiation light (W cm-2), t is the photoreaction time (s), λ is the 
wavelength of the monochromatic light (nm). 
 
Details of theoretical calculations: The structural units of the polymer fragment structures were 
optimized, using B3LYP/6-311G** to optimize in Gaussian 09. CAM-B3LYP/6-311G** was used 
for the calculation of the dipole moments. The electrostatic potential (ESP) distribution was 
constructed by the Multiwfn program and Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD).3
