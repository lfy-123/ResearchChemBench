Computational Details

     All density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out using Gaussian 16,1 employing

the hybrid B3LYP functional2 augmented with Grimme’s D3 dispersion correction using the Becke–

Johnson (BJ) damping scheme.3,4 Geometries were optimized with the SDD effective core potential5

for Ru and the 6-31G(d,p) basis set6,7 for all other atoms (S, C, N, O, H). Vibrational frequency

analyses were performed at the same level of theory to confirm the nature of each stationary point

(minimum or transition states) and to provide Gibbs free energy corrections at 298.15 K. Single-point

energies were refined using the def2-TZVP basis set8 for all atoms with the SMD continuum solvation

model9 to account for acetonitrile solvent effects.

    The experimental absolute redox potential of the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE, 4.52 V)10

was  utilized  to  calculate redox  potentials, which was  established  as −0.028(4) V11 vs  the

ferrocenium/ferrocene couple. Consequently, standard redox potentials were computed relative to the

ferrocenium/ferrocene (Fc⁺/0) couple, taken as 4.548 V in acetonitrile, corresponding to an absolute

electron affinity of 104.879 kcal/mol.

    For pKa calculations, the Gibbs free energy of a proton in acetonitrile was taken as –264.7

kcal/mol, incorporating both its experimental solvation free energy (–258.4 kcal/mol)12,13 and a gas-

phase translational entropy correction (–6.3 kcal/mol) 14. The pKa of NH₄⁺ was estimated at 15.1 when

modeled as the [NH₄⁺⋯NH₃] dimer, consistent with the experimental value (15.6) 15, and this value was

used as a pH reference. The Gibbs energy for proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) was calculated

as 390.2 kcal/mol based on the expression: GPCET = 104.879 + 264.7 + 1.364 × pH, using pH = 15.1.

Standard state corrections were applied: +1.9 kcal/mol for species undergoing a 1 atm to 1 M

concentration change, and +3.6 kcal/mol for acetonitrile, as its standard state concentration of 19.2 M.

Unless otherwise noted, all reported energies include Gibbs corrections and D3(BJ) dispersion.

Molecular structures were visualized using CYLView.16 Visual molecular dynamics (VMD) software17

and the Multiwfn program18 were used in the orbitals visualization.
