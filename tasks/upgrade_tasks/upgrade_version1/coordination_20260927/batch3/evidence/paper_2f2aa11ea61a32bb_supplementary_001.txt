Experimental section
General
Measurement
1H, 13C{1H}, 11B, and 19F NMR spectra were recorded in CDCl3 using JEOL ECA-500 and JEOL
ECZ-400. High resolution mass spectra (HRMS) were measured with a positive mode by a Bruker
micrOTOF  II spectrometer; time  of  flight technique, electrospray  ionization  (ESI). UV-vis
absorption spectra were recorded on a JASCO spectrophotometer V-750. Fluorescence emission
spectra were recorded on a JASCO fluorescence spectrophotometer FP-8550. Absolute
Fluorescence quantum yield () were measured by Labsphere 4P-GPS-040-SF with using
SHIMADZU RF-5300 as a excited light source and JASCO MV-3600 as a spectrometer. PXRD
measurement was carried out by θ−2θXRD using Cu Kα radiation (Rigaku SmartLab). A sub-
nanosecond time-resolved fluorescence system employing the time-correlated single-photon-
counting method was built based on an inverted optical microscope (IX73, Olympus Corp., Tokyo,
Japan). A pulsed 405-nm laser (FOLS-12/405, CRAFT CENTER SAWAKI Inc., Hamamatsu,
Japan) was used as the excitation source to irradiate samples. The generated fluorescence was
detected with a photon-counting detector (SPD-050-CTD, Micro Photon Device) attached to a
spectrometer (H-20, JOBIN YVON S.A.S.) using an optical fiber. The generated signal was
analyzed using a photon-counting board (TimeHarp 260 PICO; PicoQuant GmbH). The time
resolution  of  the measurements was 500  ps.  Differential  scanning  calorimetry (DSC)
measurement were performed using a DSC-Q2000 (TA Instruments, Japan) at a heating rate of

10 or 5 °C min–1.


Computational details
DFT calculations were carried out to investigate the frontier orbitals. In addition, the HOMO-LUMO
transition energies (E), their oscillator strength (f), and the energy levels of single (Sn) and triplet
(Tn) excited states were estimated by TD-DFT calculations of the frontier orbital energies and the
electron transition energies employed B3LYP/6-31G+(d,p) set combination by using Gaussian 16
program package. We confirmed from the vibrational analyses that each optimized geometry
corresponds to a local minimum that has only real frequencies.

P5Cl(Y), 1d-P5F(Y), and 1d-P6Cl
The single crystal was mounted on a LithoLoops. Intensity data were collected at 100 K on a
Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy-S (Dualflex; HyPix) with graphite monochromated Cu Kα radiation

X-ray Crystallographic Data for Single Crystalline Products; for 1d-P5Cl(LB1) and 1d-
P5F(LB)
The single crystal was mounted on a glass fiber. Intensity data were collected at 100 K on a
Rigaku XtaLAB mini with graphite monochromated Mo Kα radiation. Readout was performed in
the 0.073 mm pixel mode. The data were collected to a maximum 2θvalue of 55.0°. Data were
processed using the CrysAlisPro. An analytical numeric absorption correction was applied. The
data were corrected for Lorentz and polarization effects. The structure was solved by the ShelXT
and expanded using Fourier techniques. Non-hydrogen atoms were refined anisotropically.
Hydrogen atoms were refined using the riding model. The final cycle of full-matrix least-squares
refinement on F2 was based on observed reflections and variable parameters. All calculations
were performed using the Olex2 crystallographic software package except for refinement, which
was performed using, SHELXL2016 respectively.
