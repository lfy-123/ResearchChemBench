Experimental Materials and Methods

1.  General Information:

NMR spectra were obtained using Bruker AV300 spectrometer and Bruker AV400

spectrometer. Chemical shifts (δ) were measured in parts per million (ppm) relative to

TMS δ = 0 or chloroform δ = 7.26 for 1H and to chloroform δ = 77.16 for 13C as internal

standard. 11B{1H} NMR spectra data are referenced to external BF3·Et2O. All coupling

constants (J values) are reported in Hertz (Hz). HRMS spectra were obtained on an

Agilent 1290-6546 UHPLC Q-T of HRMS spectrometer. X-ray crystallographic

analyses were performed on an Oxford diffraction Gemini E diffractometer. Melting

points were obtained on an X-4 micromelting point apparatus. The UV−vis absorption

spectra were measured on a VARIAN Cary 60 instrument. The emission spectra were

performed on Hitachi F-4700 Fluorescence  spectrometers. Quantum  yields and

fluorescence lifetimes were measured on QYC11347 and TAUC11367. All reactions

except noted were routinely performed under an inert atmosphere of nitrogen by using

standard Schlenk techniques and dry deoxygenated solvents. THF was obtained by

distillation from Na. All commercially available reagents were used without further

purification. Silica gel (300-400 mesh) purchased from Qingdao Haiyang Chemical

Industry Co. Ltd. was used for chromatographic separations.

2.  Safety Statement:

Caution! tert-Butyllithium, n-butyllithium, and decaborane were used in the synthetic

procedures. tert-Butyllithium and n-butyllithium are extremely pyrophoric reagents

that can ignite spontaneously upon exposure to air and react violently with moisture.

All operations must be performed under an  inert atmosphere using  rigorously

anhydrous techniques, on the smallest practicable scale, and only by trained personnel

wearing appropriate PPE. A Class D fire extinguisher and inert extinguishing/absorbent

material (e.g., dry sand) should be readily available. Reactions involving alkyllithium

reagents must be quenched cautiously before  further workup. Decaborane  is a

flammable solid and is acutely neurotoxic (including fatal if inhaled) and therefore must

be handled exclusively in a well-ventilated fume hood with appropriate PPE.

Synthesis of 1 (with 1a as an example).

A vigorously stirring solution of decaborane (661mg, 5 mmol, 1 equiv) in dry toluene

(20 mL) was prepared at room temperature under a nitrogen atmosphere. Caution!

Decaborane is a flammable solid and a potential acute neurotoxin. It must be handled

in a well-ventilated fume hood to avoid inhalation or skin contact. N,N-dimethylaniline

(1.27 mL, 10 mmol, 2 equiv) was then added to the solution, which was stirred for 1 h

at room temperature. Subsequently, 1-Bromo-8-(2-phenylethynyl)naphthalene (1535

mg, 5 mmol, 1 equiv) was introduced into the mixture. The solution was warmed to

120 °C and stirred for an additional 2 h. The reaction was quenched by the addition of

water (15 mL), and the resulting mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (15 mL × 3

times). The organic phases were combined and concentrated to dryness under reduced

pressure. The resultant residue was subjected to column chromatography on silica gel

(300-400 mesh) using n-hexane as the eluent to yield the final product.

Synthesis of 2 (with 2a as an example).

To a vigorously stirring solution of 1a (424 mg, 1 mmol, 1 equiv) in dry THF (5 mL)

at -78 °C under nitrogen was added a solution of t-BuLi in hexane (1.3 M, 0.77 mL, 1

mmol, 1 equiv). Caution! tert-Butyllithium is an extremely pyrophoric reagent that

ignites spontaneously upon exposure to air and reacts violently with moisture. All

operations involving t-BuLi must be carried out under an inert atmosphere using

rigorously anhydrous conditions, and only by trained personnel with appropriate

protective equipment. The mixture was stirred at -78 °C for 2 h and then warmed to

room temperature. Then, the reaction was quenched with water (5 mL) and extracted

with ethyl acetate (5 mL × 3 times). The organic portions were combined and

concentrated to dryness in vacuo. The residue was subjected to column chromatography

on silica gel (300-400 mesh) using n-hexane as the eluent to give the product 2a.

X-ray Structure Determination.

Single crystals were immersed in Paraton-N oil and sealed under argon in thin-walled

glass capillaries. All data were collected at 296 K on a Bruker Kappa ApexII Duo

Diffractometer using Mo-Kα radiation. An empirical absorption correction was applied

using the SADABS program. All structures were solved by direct methods and

subsequent Fourier difference techniques and refined anisotropically for  all non-

hydrogen atoms by full-matrix least squares calculations on F2 using the SHELXTL

program package. All hydrogen atoms were geometrically fixed using the riding model.

Crystal data and details of data collection and refinement are provided in Tables S2-S3.
