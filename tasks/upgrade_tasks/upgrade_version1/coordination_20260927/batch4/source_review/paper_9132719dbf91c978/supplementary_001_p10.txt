0.36 mmol), DMA (2 mL) and PhSiH3 (1.1 mL, 8.92 mmol). Tube B was filled with

1,2-diphenylethene (36.0 mg, 0.20 mmol), 10 w.t.% Pd/C (21.3 mg, 0.02 mmol) and

Two chamber reaction without photoirradiation

Two 25 mL Schlenk tubes loaded with stirring bars were connected by a double headed

needle. Tube A was filled with Pd2(dba)3 (163.4 mg, 0.18 mmol), XantPhos (206.3 mg,

0.36 mmol), DMA (2 mL) and PhSiH3 (1.1 mL, 8.92 mmol). Tube B was filled with

1,2-diphenylethene (36.0 mg, 0.20 mmol), 10 w.t.% Pd/C (21.3 mg, 0.02 mmol) and

MeOH (2 mL). After Tube A was stirred for 16 h, 1,2-diphenylethene in Tube B was

unreacted (no 1,2-diphenylethane was detected by GC–MS).


4. Computational Methods and Results

4.1 Computational Methods

  All the calculations were performed on the Gaussian 16 (A.03) program package.[15]
Considering the applicability of PBE0 functional on computing the energy barriers of
organic and organometallic reactions in solution,[16] the hybrid PBE0[17] functional in
conjugation with the def2-SVP basis set[18] and the D3 version of Grimme’s dispersion
correction  with  Becke-Johnson  damping  (GD3-BJ)[19] was  used  for  structure
optimization. The corresponding frequency analysis at the same level was conducted to
characterize the nature of the optimized structures; i.e., energy minimums without
imaginary frequencies or transition states with only one imaginary frequency; and the
imaginary model  connects  the  initial  and  the  final  states. On  the  basis  of
PBE0(GD3BJ)/def2-SVP-optimized geometries, MN15 functional[20] in conjugation
with the def2-TZVP basis set were used to calculate the self-consistent reaction field
(SCRF) single-point energies in the solvation of N,N-dmethylacetamide (MN15-
SCRF(SCRF)/def2-TZVP//PBE0/def2-SVP) by using the SMD solvation model.[21]
Graphics with 3D rending in ball-and-stick model was drawn in CYLview 1.0
visualization software.[22] The spin density on INT-C was analyzed by the wavefunction
analysis program Multiwfn 3.8.[23]
