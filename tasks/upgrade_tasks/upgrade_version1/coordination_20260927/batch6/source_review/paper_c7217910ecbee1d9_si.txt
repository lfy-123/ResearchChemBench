Shell-independent superhalogen formation in aluminum-based clusters via boron Lewis acid ligands functionalization 
 
Computational details
Within the framework of density functional theory, geometrical optimizations and electronic structure calculations were performed for all naked and ligated aluminum-based superatomic clusters, as well as their associated coupled complexes. These calculations utilized the PBE0 [1] hybrid functional with the def2-SVP [2] basis set, as implemented in the Gaussian 16 package [3]. The feasibility of this theoretical method on the geometries and electronic structures of aluminum-based clusters has been verified in previous literatures [4-7]. Various possible spin multiplicities and binding positions of the ligands were examined to identify the global minima of both naked and ligated clusters. Frequency analysis was conducted at the same level of theory to confirm the absence of imaginary frequencies, and zero-point energy corrections were also incorporated. To further validate the choice of the functional and basis sets used in this study, the adiabatic electron affinity (AEA) of Al13 and the adiabatic ionization potential (AIP) of PAl12 were calculated to be approximately 3.54 eV and 5.25 eV, respectively. These values are consistent with the experimental measurements of 3.57 ± 0.05 eV [8] and 5.37 ± 0.04 eV [9], respectively. Additionally, the structures of PAl12[B(C6F5)3]n (n = 1-2) and the neutral and anionic MAl12[B(C6F5)3]n@graphene (M = Al, C, and P; n = 0, 2) were optimized using the projector augmented wave (PAW) method [10] and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [11] functional, as implemented in the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP) [12]. The plane-wave cutoff energy was set to 500 eV, with convergence criteria for total energy and atomic forces set at 10-5 eV and 0.02 eV/Å, respectively. To account for long-range van der Waals (vdW) interactions, Grimme’s DFT-D3 method [13] was employed. A unit cell with a vacuum space of 20 Å in all three dimensions was utilized to prevent virtual interactions, and the first Brillouin zone was sampled using the Γ-point. Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) [14] simulations were also performed to evaluate the thermal stability of the PAl12[B(C6F5)3]n (n = 1-2) clusters, employing a canonical (NVT) ensemble with the Nosé heat bath method [15] at 300 K for 10 ps, utilizing a time step of 2 fs.
To demonstrate the applicability and versatility of the BLAs ligand strategy in constructing superhalogens, we expanded the scope of our model cluster from aluminum-based clusters to gold-based clusters. The BP86 functional [16] was adopted to optimize the naked and ligated gold-based superatomic clusters. Here, the LANL2DZ basis set [17] with the relativistic effective core potential (ECP) was used to describe the Au atomic orbital, while the def2-SVP basis set was employed for the B, C, and F atoms. The AEA of Au20 was calculated to be 2.91 eV, which is in good agreement with the experimental value (2.75 ± 0.02 eV) [18].
The Hirshfeld charge was analyzed to estimate the amount of charge transfer between the ligand and the metallic core in ligated aluminum-based clusters. The electrostatic potential (ESP) analysis, molecular orbital (MO) analysis, and charge decomposition analysis (CDA) were also conducted and visualized via the Multiwfn [19] and VMD [20] software. The structures of the clusters were generated using CYLview [21].
