                                                                                 3.7. Comparative study

                                                                The INP compound’s nonlinear refractive index, as determined by
                                                                        the SSPM, was around 10⁻6 cm²/W. The Z-scan technique test revealed
                                                                             that it was around 10⁻8 cm²/W. This two-order-of-magnitude disparity is
                                                                              in accordance with previous results for similar nonlinear optical systems
                                                             composed of materials. For instance, Z-scan studies on comparable dye
                                                                          materials often provide values between 10⁻7 and 10⁻8 cm²/W [12,48,
                                                                          49], whereas diffraction-based measurements for dyes typically reveal
                                                                           n₂ values between 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁷ cm²/W [18,50,51]. These patterns in the
                                                                                  literature attest to the fact that thermal lensing and cumulative phase
                                                                           contributions cause the  diffraction technique to produce a  larger

3.6. Temporal evolution of diffraction rings measurements

    Fig. 9a illustrates the temporal evolution of the diffraction rings of
the INP compound at varying concentrations using the SSPM technique,
with an input laser power of 50 mW. Figs. 9b and (c) illustrate the
vertical and horizontal radii of diffraction rings as a function of the laser
beam’s duration (even though some of the images of the diffraction rings
were not shown in Fig. 9a). We observe that each ring design loses
symmetry in the vertical direction compared to the horizontal direction
over time. The changes in the self-diffraction rings over time show that
they start to distort at different times for concentrations of 0.25, 0.5, and
1.0 mM. This results in significant distortion in the DR rings. Throughout
the growth phases, the vertical compression impact induced by heat
convection is the most significant [47], as seen in Fig. (9b and c). The
