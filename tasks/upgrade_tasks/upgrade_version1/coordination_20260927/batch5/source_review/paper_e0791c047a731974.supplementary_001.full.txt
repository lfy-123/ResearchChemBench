integrating sphere, SM4 from Edinburgh Instruments Ltd.), and transient PL spectra

2.4 Upconversion film fabrication

Substrate Pretreatment: Glass substrates of two sizes (1.7 cm × 1.7 cm × 0.7 mm

and 5.0 cm × 5.0 cm × 2.0 mm) were ultrasonically cleaned sequentially in ethanol,

deionized water, acetone, and chloroform, then dried under an argon stream and
=== PDF PAGE 6 ===
transferred into an argon-filled glovebox.2

Solution Preparation: Inside the glovebox, Cy2 (0.1 mM) and rubrene (50 mM) were

dissolved in argon-saturated chloroform under stirring until a homogeneous mixture

was obtained.

Spin-Coating: A 15 μL aliquot of the mixture was deposited vertically onto the center

of a 1.7 cm × 1.7 cm × 0.7 mm glass substrate spinning at 2500 rpm, followed by

spin-coating for 60 seconds to allow complete solvent evaporation, yielding a uniform

upconversion thin film.

Encapsulation: The coated substrate was placed in the center of a recessed 5.0 cm ×

5.0 cm ×  2.0 mm glass substrate, and the edges were sealed with a UV-curable

acrylate-based epoxy resin. Care was taken to minimize bubble formation during

sealing. The final sample was stored in the glovebox until use.



2.5 Measurement of femtosecond transient absorption (fs-TA) spectra


    The femtosecond transient absorption (fs-TA) spectra of IR780, Cy1, Cy2, and

Cy3 were acquired using an ultrafast transient absorption fluorescence microscopic

spectroscopy system (PH-Tuning + TA100). The samples were prepared in a glovebox,

dissolved in degassed chloroform at appropriate concentrations, and loaded into a 1 cm

× 2 mm quartz cuvette for measurement. The pump wavelength was set to 750 nm with

an excitation power of 90 – 300 μW. The detection time window ranged from -1 ps to

7000  ps, and  the  spectral  acquisition range covered 500 – 1030 nm. Spectra

measurements were performed in an inert environment.

2.8 Theoretical calculation


     All electronic structure calculation and vibrational analysis of IR780, Cy1, Cy2

and Cy3 (excluding iodide ions) on ground and excited states were performed by using

Gaussian 16 package at B3LYP/6-31g(d,p) level with SMD model for solvation in

chloroform.3-6 It is noteworthy that we adopt the pseudopotential basis set SDD for
=== PDF PAGE 8 ===
iodine atoms and def2TZVP for selenium atoms to make the calculations more

accurate.7-9 The Density Functional Theory (DFT) was employed for the ground state

(S0) optimization, while Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory (TDDFT) was

used for vertical excitation and geometric optimization on S1, T1, and T2 states. The

electron-hole analysis and electron-hole heat maps of low-lying excited states was

performed by using Multiwfn program,10 while the resulted electron-hole distributions

were further visualized by GaussView program.3 According to Fermi’s golden rule,

the ISC rate is proportional to the square of the spin-orbit coupling (SOC) matrix

element. Since the SOC constant scales with the fourth power of the atomic number,

the enhanced SOC in Cy2 is a key factor responsible for its markedly higher ISC rate

compared to Cy1.



2.9 Measurement of TTA-UC spectra

   By using solid-state laser illumination at 830 nm, it was possible to determine the

upconversion spectra of IR780/rubrene, Cy1/rubrene, Cy2/rubrene, Cy3/rubrene, and

IR806/rubrene in argon-saturated chloroform (Excitation power: 1.3 W, slit: 0.1 nm,

PMT900 detector). Additionally, the upconversion spectra of Cy2/rubrene thin film

(1.7 cm×1.7 cm×0.7 mm) were measured in the Edinburgh FLS1000 spectrometer

under 830 nm excitation after encapsulation with a 5cm × 5cm×2 mm glass plate.


2.10 Correction of TTA-UC spectra


    Due to the high concentration of rubrene in chloroform, we corrected the TTA-UC

spectra with the fluorescence spectra of rubrene in diluted solution and obtained the

corrected TTA-UC spectra excluding the inner-filtering effect.11 Specifically, we

multiplied the intensity ratio of the TTA-UC spectra to the dilute solution rubrene

spectra at 585 nm, where the influence of inner-filtering effects is negligible, to the

latter spectra, and thus simulated the corrected TTA-UC spectra. The consistency of the
=== PDF PAGE 9 ===
spectra from 585 nm to 750 nm before and after correction confirmed the feasibility of

our simulations, and the difference in the spectra before 585 nm was the result of the

inner-filtering effect.

2.13 The upconversion lifetime measurements

    The IR780/rubrene, Cy1/rubrene, and Cy2/rubrene samples were prepared in a
