1. Computational Methods

Coordinates for structures were generated before being subjected to CREST1 conformational
sampling with the GFN2-xTB2 method. Of the conformers generated, the lowest energy axial and
equatorial structure was selected and used in following calculations. All DFT calculations were
performed with Gaussian16.3 Structures were optimized and frequency and NBO calculations
were performed at (GD3)4B3LYP5/Def2TZVPP6 in the gas phase. NBO version 3.17 was used.
Single  point energies and coupling constants  of  1H,  13C, and  119Sn were calculated  at
(GD3)B3LYP5/TZP-ZORA8  with  orbital  coefficients  taken from  the  basis  set  exchange.9
nmr=(spinspin,mixed,readatoms) was used to calculated heteronuclear spin-spin coupling only
for the desired atoms to reduce computational cost. Single point energies reported in Table S1
were taken from the B3LYP/TZP-ZORA calculations. A subscript “Bu” refers to the average of the
carbon bonded to the tin in each of the butyl groups.

Calculations using the TZP-ZORA basis set led to the “Inaccurate quadrature in CalDSu”
Gaussian error message. This was corrected with the integral=NoXCTest keyword.

2. Benchmark Regression Analysis





Figure S1. Regression analysis of data in Table 1: regression between experimental and
calculated  1J(Cano-H)  values  for  identical  structures,  and  regression  between
experimental and calculated 1J(Cano-H) values for tin-substituted structures.

To indicate the chosen method can reproduce experimentally derived coupling constants
in cyclic molecules, a set of structures with known 1J(C-H) values were optimized and had
coupling constants calculated, data shown in Figure S1. J-values were reproduced with
high fidelity to experiment, having a max error of 9 Hz, and an MAE of 5 Hz. For the
corresponding tin-substituted structures, the model begins to lose predictive capability,
with max error increasing to 22 Hz and MAE increasing to 8 Hz. This is likely due to the
perturbation of the anomeric carbon with an attached heavy tin atom.
