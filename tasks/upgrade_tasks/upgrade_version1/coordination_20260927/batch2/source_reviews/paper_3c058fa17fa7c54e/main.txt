A B S T R A C T
The triplet-triplet annihilation (TTA) mechanism, which converts low-energy triplet excitons into singlet exci­
tons, has been successfully applied to manufacture high-performance organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs). 
TTA-type materials usually exhibited weak intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect, which are advantageous 
for designing deep-blue emitters. Herein, we reported two newly developed TTA molecules, An-σ-Ph and An- 
σ-DA, based on anthracene core by a σ-bond unit strategy to weak the intramolecular conjugation degree. Their 
molecular structures, and thermochemical/photophysical/electrochemical properties have been systematically 
studied in both experimental and theoretical aspects. These molecules achieved deep-blue emission with high 
PLQYs in solution and thin films. As a result, non-doped OLEDs based on An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA achieved a 
maximum external quantum efficiency (EQEmax) and a maximum luminance (Lmax) of 3.26 %/4398 cd m−2 and 
3.43 %/12870 cd m−2, respectively, displaying a deep-blue emission with a peak electroluminescence at 447/ 
438 nm. Furthermore, the EQE roll-off of An-σ-DA-based device can be ignored at 1000 cd m−2 and maintain 
good device performances at 10000 cd m−2. This study provides a new avenue for designing novel deep-blue TTA 
emitters for efficient non-doped OLEDs.
1. Introduction
Organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) are widely regarded as the 
next-generation technology in the fields of display and solid-state 
lighting due to their unique advantages [1–4], such as light weight, 
low energy consumption, and short response time, etc. Blue emission is 
one of the three primary colors of light and is the foundation for creating 
full-color flat-panel displays and white-light illumination [5–7]. 
Accordingly, the development of high-performance blue emission ma­
terials is a priority in OLEDs field. In the past decades, many efforts have 
been made to obtain efficient blue OLEDs [3,8–12]. However, deep-blue 
emission materials have a large optical bandgap (Eg), which is not 
conducive to injection and transport of charge carriers, resulting in 
inferior electroluminescence (EL) performances [13–15]. Therefore, the 
high-performance deep-blue OLEDs are rarely observed in practice.
The initial exploration was focused on traditional fluorescent (CF) 
materials, yet their internal quantum efficiency (IQE) was below 25 % 
[3,16–18]. To achieve higher IQE, phosphorescent (PH), triplet-triplet 
annihilation (TTA) and thermally activated delayed fluorescence 
(TADF) materials have been proposed [19–21]. PH materials usually 
require precious metal coordination and show high cost that are not 
conducive to commercial applications [22,23]. Although outstanding 
device performances were achieved with TADF emitters-based OLEDs 
[10,24–26], most TADF materials possess strong intramolecular charge 
transfer (ICT) effect which results in the emission being located in the 
sky-blue or green region [4,27–29]. Compared to TADF materials, TTA 
emitters can obtain 62.5 % of IQE by converting two triplet excitons into 
singlet excitons through collision, and have weak ICT effect [2,13,30], 
which is beneficial for constructing deep-blue to blue OLEDs.
Anthracene (AN) has been frequently used as core or peripheral 
functional unit to design luminescent materials [2,13,31–33]. Obvi­
ously, this fact is attributed to their superior photoluminescence quan­
tum yield (PLQY), electrochemical stability, and thermal stability. For 
example, in 2022, Xie et al. used AN as a modification group for pyrene

non-doped OLEDs.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Synthesis and characterization
As shown in Scheme 1, An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were obtained through 
a similar synthetic route. The reaction intermediates (A1 ~ A2 and B1 ~ 
B2) were synthesized according to the reported literatures [2,13,35], 
and the final substances were synthesized by the Suzuki-Miyaura reac­
tion with good yields. The molecular skeleton of the two compounds 
were characterized by 1H NMR spectra, 13C NMR spectra, and mass 
spectrometry (see Supporting Information Fig. S1-S6).
2.2. Theoretical calculations
Optimized molecular skeleton and frontline molecular orbitals dis­
tribution for An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were obtained by density functional 
theory (DFT) calculation under the B3LYP/6-31G (d, p) basis set. The 
optimized structures of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA are shown in Fig. 2. It is 
obvious that the large twist angle between both AN and σ-bond unit can 
effectively suppress ACQ effect and emission red-shifts. Consequently, 
the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoc­
cupied molecular orbital (LUMO) are distributed on different AN unit, 
demonstrating that the introduction of σ-bond unit effectively disrupts 
the intramolecular conjugation. The theoretical values of HOMO and 
LUMO for An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA are −1.71/-5.20 eV and −1.97/-5.20 
eV, respectively. To gain deeper insight into the photophysical proper­
ties at the molecular level, we computed the ultraviolet absorption (UV) 
spectra under the HF/6-31G (d, p) basis set. These calculations aim to 
correlate the electronic structure of the compounds with their observed 
optical properties. As shown in Fig. S7, the introduction of methoxy and 
cyano group results in measurable shifts in the simulated UV spectrum, 
with corresponding Eg of 3.04 and 3.01 eV for An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA, 
respectively, which the potential of deep-blue emission has been proven. 
These spectral changes imply that the substituents directly influence the

designated as PAPF and PAPE, were recently reported by Chou et al. 
[37]. The structural distinction between them emerges from the use of 
either a fluorene or an ether unit serving as the linker for the two AN 
group. Despite exhibiting a slight red-shift in its non-doped film with a 
significantly suppressed shoulder peak, PAPE suffered a sharp decline in 
PLQY to 29 %. For PAPF, the observed significant emission red-shift and 
the consequent reduction in PLQY to 15 % in its non-doped film are 
attributed to the formation of excimer facilitated by the fluorene units. 
Owing to the hexafluoro-2,2-diphenylpropane linker, which introduces 
greater steric hindrance than the groups in PAPF and PAPE, An-σ-Ph 
and An-σ-DA effectively weaken intermolecular interactions, thus 
maintaining deep-blue emission and higher PLQYs. In addition, the PL 
spectra of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were tested in n-hexane (n-Hex), Tol, 
tetrahydrofuran (THF) and dichloromethane (DCM) solution, respec­
tively, showing slight red-shifts and demonstrating that their ICT effects 
are very weak. The stronger ICT effect of An-σ-DA (Δλem = 16 nm) 
compared to An-σ-Ph (Δλem = 5 nm) is caused by push-pull electron 
interaction between anisole (donor unit, D) and benzonitrile (acceptor 
unit, A) (see Table 2).
3.1. Triplet-triplet annihilation characteristic
As shown in Fig. 4, the PL intensities of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were 
enhanced after nitrogen bubbling, implying the existence of oxygen- 
sensitive triplet exciton. Furthermore, both compounds show a clear 
double-exponential decay curve in transient fluorescence spectroscopy 
and up-conversion process can be confirmed. The relevant experimental 
results and related parameters are placed in Fig. 4 and Table 1. The 
prompt fluorescence (τP) and delayed fluorescence (τD) lifetimes of An- 
σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were simulated to be 15.7 ns/24.3 μs and 12.8 ns/ 
42.7 μs, respectively. Combining the simulation results of 2T1 > S1 and 
triplet excitons participated in luminescence process, it can be deter­
mined that both emitters are characterized by TTA luminescence 
mechanism.
3.2. Thermochemical and electrochemical properties
In order to fabricate OLEDs by vacuum deposition method, the ma­
terial should have good thermal stability which can prevent decompo­
sition at high temperature. Therefore, the thermal stability of these 
materials was checked by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and dif­
ferential scanning calorimetry (DSC) under nitrogen atmosphere. As 
shown in Fig. 5, the result shows that decomposition temperatures (Td, 
temperature at which 5 % of weight is lost) of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA are 
427 and 418 ◦C, respectively. Furthermore, the glass-transition tem­
perature (Tg) of An-σ-DA was measured at 110 ◦C. In contrast, no 
distinct glass transition was observed for An-σ-Ph within the investi­
gated temperature range of 30–300 ◦C. Within the investigated tem­
perature range, no distinct melting transitions were observed for either 
An-σ-Ph or An-σ-DA. The corresponding DSC curves are provided in 
Fig. S8. Due to the presence of methoxy and cyano functional groups, not 
only is the molecular weight increased, but the degree of conjugation 
within the molecule is also enhanced. These factors collectively 
contribute to a higher complete thermal decomposition for An-σ-DA. As 
a result, a comparative analysis of the thermal profiles reveals a more 
gradual decomposition for An-σ-DA relative to An-σ-Ph upon heating. 
Both materials have good thermal stability, demonstrating that the 
introduction of σ-bond to disrupt molecular conjugation has no signifi­
cant effect on the thermal stability.
The HOMO and LUMO energy levels of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were 
determined by cyclic voltammetry (CV) test. Herein, in order to calcu­
late the HOMO energy level value of substances, the HOMO energy level 
of ferrocene is used as a referent. The first oxidation onset potentials 
(Eox) of An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA were measured at −1.08 and −1.14 eV, 
respectively, corresponding to HOMO energy levels of −5.46 and −5.38 
eV. Subsequently, the LUMO energy levels were calculated to be −2.43 
and −2.34 eV for An-σ-Ph and An-σ-DA, respectively, using the equa­
tion Eg = EHOMO - ELUMO. Compared to An-σ-Ph, introducing D and A 
unit into An-σ-DA leads to a slight increase in ICT effect and a decrease 
in the energy levels of the frontier molecular orbitals.

strategy for designing deep-blue TTA emitters and non-doped OLEDs.
4. Conclusions
In summary, this work presents two new TTA emitters, An-σ-Ph and 
An-σ-DA, which were developed by strategically introducing σ-bond 
unit to disrupt intramolecular conjugation degree. These materials are 
efficiently synthesized with good yields and exhibit distorted spatial 
configurations. Thanks to the introduction of σ-bond unit, these mate­
rials exhibit decent PLQYs and deep-blue emission with peaks of around 
428–447 nm in both photoluminescence and electroluminescence. And 
effectively suppressed the red-shift of molecules in electroluminescence 
display. The corresponding non-doped OLEDs based on An-σ-Ph and 
An-σ-DA demonstrated the EQEmax and Lmax of 3.26 %/4398 cd m−2 and 
3.43 %/12870 cd m−2, respectively, with corresponding EL peak at 447 
nm and 438 nm. Especially, the device based on An-σ-DA shows good 
stability, with a roll-off <1 % at 1000 cd m−2. This work not only 
demonstrates the successful use of σ-bond unit as an alternative strategy 
to develop high-performance deep-blue TTA emitters, but also provides 
an efficient approach for tackling challenges in deep-blue non-doped EL 
devices.
