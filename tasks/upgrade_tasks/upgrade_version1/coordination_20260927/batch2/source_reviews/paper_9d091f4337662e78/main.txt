ABSTRACT: Four new pyrazine derivatives, namely (S,E)-1-((5-(3-
hydroxy-2-(hydroxymethyl)styryl)-3,6-dimethylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-
dihydroisobenzofuran-4-ol
(1),
(S,E)-1-((5-(3-hydroxy-2-
(methoxymethyl)styryl)-3,6-dimethylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-dihydroi-
sobenzofuran-4-ol (2), (S,Z)-1-((5-(3-hydroxy-2-(methoxymethyl)-
styryl)-3,6-dimethylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-4-ol
(3), and (S)-1-((5-(3-hydroxy-2-(methoxymethyl)phenethyl)-3,6-dime-
thylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-4-ol (4), together
with one new 1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran derivative, (S)-2-(4-hydroxy-
1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-1-yl)acetamide (5), were isolated from the
endophytic fungus Alternaria sp. HJT-Y7 collected from the leaves of
Rhodiola tibetica. Their structures were determined based on extensive
spectroscopic analysis and comparison of experimental and calculated electronic circular dichroism (ECD) curves. 1, 4, and 5
significantly inhibited osteoclast differentiation in RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells and markedly restored bone mineralization in a
dexamethasone-induced zebrafish osteoporosis model. These results highlight the potential of 1, 4, and 5 as promising candidates for
osteoporosis therapy.
Osteoporosis (OP) is a metabolic bone disease commonly seen
in the elderly, characterized by decreased bone mass,
deterioration of bone microstructure, and increased bone
fragility, which significantly raises the risk of fractures.1−3
Bone homeostasis is regulated by the balance between
osteoblast and osteoclast activity. Abnormal multinucleated
osteoclast formation leads to bone homeostasis disruption, and
interventions targeting the imbalance in osteoclast formation
and activation are considered effective for treating osteopo-
rosis.4 Receptor activator of nuclear factor κB ligand (RANKL)
plays a crucial role in osteoclast development and activation.5
Current representative antiosteoclast drugs, such as bisphos-
phate, can cause severe complications with long-term use,
including osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral
fractures.6,7 Another example, denosumab, is associated with a
rebound effect upon discontinuation, resulting in decreased
bone density.8 Therefore, the search for natural active molecules
with high efficacy and low toxicity has become an important
direction for the development of innovative osteoporosis
treatments.
Fungal natural products have underscored their potential as
promising lead compounds for the development of osteoporosis
therapeutics, including macrolides,9 quinolone,10 tanzawaic
acids,11 diterpenoids,12 indole alkaloids,13 and azaphilones.14
In our ongoing search for discover novel compounds from
endophytic fungi, four previously undescribed pyrazine
derivatives (1−4), together with a new 1,3-dihydroisobenzofur-
an derivative, (S)-2-(4-hydroxy-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-1-
yl)acetamide (5), were isolated from the endophytic fungus
Alternaria sp. HJT-Y7, obtained from the leaves of Rhodiola
tibetica. Several compounds were found to inhibit RANKL-
induced osteoclastogenesis, indicating their potential as lead

(S,Z)-1-((5-(3-hydroxy-2-(methoxymethyl)styryl)-3,6-dime-
thylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-4-ol (3)
was obtained as a white amorphous powder with a molecular
formula of C25H26O4N2, determined from its HRESIMS ion
peak at m/z 419.1949 [M + H]+ (calcd. for C25H27O4N2,
419.1965), indicating 14 degrees of unsaturation and suggesting
that 3 was an isomer of 2. A comparison of the 1D NMR data of
3 and 2 revealed that they were highly similar, with the only
difference being the significant upfield shift of H-10 (δH 6.76)
and H-11 (δH 7.03) in 3 compared to 2. The Z-configuration of
the C-10/C-11 double bond was readily assigned based on the
smaller coupling constant (J = 10.0 Hz) between H-10 and H-
11. To further confirm the absolute configuration of 3, ECD
calculations were performed at the CAM-B3LYP/6-31 +
G(2d,p) level. As shown in Figure 2, the calculated ECD
spectrum of (1′S)-3 closely matched the experimental ECD
data, leading to the assignment of the absolute configuration of 3
as 1′S.
(S)-1-((5-(3-hydroxy-2-(methoxymethyl)phenethyl)-3,6-di-
methylpyrazin-2-yl)methyl)-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-4-ol (4)
was isolated as a white amorphous powder. Its molecular
formula, was deduced by the pseudomolecular ion peak at m/z
421.2114 [M + H]+ (calcd. for C25H29O4N2, 421.2122) in the
HRESIMS spectrum. The 1H and 13C NMR spectra of 4 closely
resembled those of 2, except for the presence of two methylene
groups (δH 2.95, 2.95), and the absence of two olefinic carbons.
This assignment was further supported by the HMBC
correlations from H-10 to C-11 and C-1″, from H-11 to C-5,
C-1″, C-2″, and C-6″, from H2−7″ to C-1″, C-2″, and C-3″,
from OH-7″ to C-2″, and from OH-3″ to C-2″, C-3″, and C-4″
(Figure 1). The closely matching ECD curve (Figure 2)
provided strong evidence that the absolute configuration of 4
(1′S) was identical to that of 2.
(S)-2-(4-hydroxy-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-1-yl)acetamide
(5) was yielded as a white amorphous powder. The molecular
formula of 5 was determined to be C10H11NO3 based on the
HRESIMS analysis, which revealed a [M + Na]+ ion at m/z
216.0633 (calcd. for C10H11NO3Na, 216.0631). The 1D and 2D
NMR spectroscopic data of 5 were nearly identical to those of 3-
deoxyisoochracinic acid. HMBC correlation (Figure 1) from
NH2-9 (δH 7.31, 6.89) to C-8 (δC 42.5) confirmed the presence
of an amide group at C-9. The structure appeared to represent
half of the backbone of 1. The absolute configuration of 5 was
established as (1′S) by ECD calculations (Figure 2).
Currently, pyrazine-type natural products derived from fungi
are rare, and are primarily found in species of Aspergillus15 and
Talaromyces.16 These compounds typically feature simple
pyrazin-2(1H)-one or pyrazine cores. In subsequent studies,
the NRPS module (NRPS325) of the only PKS-NRPS
megasynthetase (ATEG00325) in Aspergillus terreus was found
to be capable of synthesizing thiol-substituted pyrazines,17
providing partial insight into the biosynthetic logic of pyrazine-
type natural products from fungi. In contrast, compounds 1−4
exhibit more novel structures, as they are derived from two
transaminated polyketide monomers, namely (dihydroisoben-
zofuran-1-yl)acetamide (5) and its structural analogs. Through

To assess the antiosteoporosis activity of 1−5, the receptor
activator of nuclear factor κB ligand (RANKL) -induced
RAW264.7 cell was employed. As shown in Figure 3A,B, the
increased cell volume and tartrate resistant acid phosphatase
(TRAP) staining observed in the model (MOD) group. In
comparison with the MOD group, 1, 4 and 5 effectively
inhibited the osteoclast differentiation and decreased the
number of osteoclasts. The results indicated that 1, 4 and 5
exhibit antiosteoporosis activity. To further evaluate the
antiosteoporosis activity in vivo, we established a dexametha-
sone-induced zebrafish osteoporosis model. Alizarin red staining
revealed that the staining area was significantly reduced in MOD
group, which compared with the control (CON) group, and
treatment with 1, 4, and 5 significantly restored the staining area
(Figure 3C,D). The above results demonstrated the anti-
osteoporosis efficacy of 1, 4, and 5 in vitro and in vivo.
Additionally, as shown in Figure S39, none of the compounds 1,
4, and 5 tested displayed cytotoxicity at a concentration of 100
μM. In contrast, 2 exhibited no antiosteoporosis activity,
suggesting that the hydroxyl group at C-7″ may be crucial.
When the C-7″ position was oxygen-methylated, the Δ10 double
bond appeared somewhat unnecessary for activity. Antiosteopo-
rosis activity was restored only when the Δ10 double bond was
reduced, which also explains why 2 and 3 lack activity, while 4
remains active.
In summary, four novel pyrazines and one related biosynthetic
analog from Alternaria sp. HJT-Y7. The structures and absolute
configurations of these compounds were determined through a
combination of NMR, HRESIMS, and ECD spectroscopic data.
Notably, three of them (1, 4, and 5) suppressed osteoclast
differentiation in RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells and
significantly enhanced bone mineralization in a dexametha-
sone-induced zebrafish model of osteoporosis, highlighting its
potential as a promising lead for osteolytic disease treatment.
These findings expand the chemical and bioactive diversity of
pyrazines, providing a foundation for their further investigation
as potential therapeutic agents for osteoporosis and for the
exploration of their biosynthetic pathways.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures
Optical rotations were determined using a Rudolph Autopol IV
polarimeter (Rudolph Research Analytical, Flanders, NJ, USA) at 20
°C. Ultraviolet (UV) spectra were recorded on a Techcomp UV2600
spectrophotometer (Techcomp Group, Beijing, China). IR spectrum
was recorded on a Thermo Scientific Nicolete iS20 Fourier transform
infrared (FT-IR) spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA,
USA). High-resolution electrospray ionization mass spectra (HRE-
SIMS) were acquired on an AB Sciex Triple TOF 4600 system (AB
SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA). Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)
spectra, including 1H, 13C, and 2D experiments, were recorded on a
Bruker Avance II 500 MHz spectrometer (Bruker, Karlsruhe,
Germany). Chemical shifts (δ) are reported in ppm, with coupling
constants (J) expressed in Hz, and were referenced to the residual
solvent signal of DMSO-d6. Circular dichroism (CD) spectra were
measured using a Jasco J-810−150S spectropolarimeter (JASCO
Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Semipreparative high-performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC) was carried out on Agilent 1260 Infinity
(Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) and Shimadzu LC-
20AR (Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) systems equipped with a
Welch Ultimate XB-C18 column (250 mm × 10.0 mm, 5 μm). For
column chromatography (CC), silica gel (100−200 and 200−300
mesh; Qingdao Marine Chemical Co., Ltd., Qingdao, China) and
Sephadex LH-20 (GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences AB, Uppsala, Sweden)
were employed. Analytical and preparative thin-layer chromatography
(TLC) was performed on precoated silica gel GF254 plates (Qingdao
Marine Chemistry Co., Ltd., Qingdao, China).
Fungal Strain and Fermentation
The fungal strain HJT-Y7 was obtained from the leaves of R. tibetica.
Based on sequence analysis of the ITS1−5.8S−ITS2 region of the
rDNA (GenBank accession number: PP941786), the isolate was
identified as Alternaria sp. The strain has been deposited at the Institute
of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CGMCC No. 40166),
and a voucher specimen is maintained at the College of Life and Health,
Dalian University, Dalian, China.
For cultivation, the fungus was initially grown on potato dextrose
agar (PDA; potato starch 4 g/L, dextrose 20 g/L, agar 15 g/L) plates at
28 °C for 5 days. The resulting mycelial mats were cut into small
fragments and transferred into 250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing
100 mL of potato dextrose broth (PDB; potato starch 4 g/L, dextrose
20 g/L). The cultures were incubated on a rotary shaker (120 rpm) at
28 °C for 3 days to prepare the seed inoculum. Large-scale fermentation
was subsequently carried out in one hundred 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks,
each containing 80 g of rice and 110 mL of distilled water, and
incubated at 28 °C under static conditions for 40 days.

ECD Calculation Methods
The initial conformational analysis of compounds 1−5 was carried out
using the Spartan’14 software, applying the MMFF94 molecular
mechanics force field.18 Conformers within a 10 kcal/mol energy
window were subsequently optimized using density functional theory
(DFT) at the M06-2X-GD3/6-311G(d,p) level with Gaussian 09.19
Frequency calculations at the same level were performed to confirm
that the conformers corresponded to true local minima. The more
accurate energies of the optimized conformers were calculated at the
M06-2X-GD3/6-311 + G(2d,p) level of theory in the gas phase.
Thermal corrections to the Gibbs free energies, derived from frequency
analyses, were then incorporated to yield the final Gibbs free energies
for each conformer.
Boltzmann populations at 298.15 K were calculated, and conformers
with populations greater than 1% were selected for further
investigation. ECD spectra were calculated using time-dependent
DFT (TD-DFT) at the CAM-B3LYP/6-31 + G(2d,p) level of theory
(MeOH, IEFPCM solvent model), and the results were Boltzmann-
averaged and visualized using SpecDis 1.70.1 software.20
Osteoclast Differentiation Inhibitory Assay
RAW264.7 cells were seeded into 96-well plates and induced with 100
ng/mL RANKL for 5 days in the presence or absence of 10 μM
compounds 1−5 (37 °C, 5% CO2). The cells were then fixed with 4%
paraformaldehyde for TRAP staining. Osteoclasts (TRAP-positive cells
with three or more nuclei) were photographed and counted under a
microscope.
Bone Mineralization Quantification Analysis
Detailed zebrafish feeding protocols were followed as previously
described.21 Briefly, 4 days postfertilization (dpf) larvae were randomly
assigned to the CON, MOD, and treatment groups (n = 6 per group).
The larvae were exposed to dexamethasone (10 μM) for 5 days. At 9
dpf, the larvae were collected and subjected to Alizarin Red S staining.
The heads of the larvae were placed on slides, and dorsal-view images of
the skulls were captured using a microscope. The area of Alizarin Red S
staining was quantified using ImageJ software.
Cell Viability Assay
Cell viability was evaluated using the MTT assay. RAW264.7 cells were
plated in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h, followed by treatment
with gradient-diluted test compounds for an additional 24 h. MTT
reagents were then added to each well, and the cells were incubated for
4 h. Absorbance at 490 nm was measured using a Feyond-MF200
microplate spectrophotometer. The experiments data were reported as
mean ± standard error of the mean (Figure S39).
