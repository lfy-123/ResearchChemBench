1. Experimental Methods
1.1 Synthesis of Chiral PDIs and Crystal Growth
(S)- and (R)-1-Phenylethylamine were purchased from Adamas without further
purification. For the synthesis of (S/R)-ClPDI-Ph, 1,6,7,12-Tetrachloroperylene
tetracarboxylic dianhydride (9.43 mmol), (S/R)-4Cl-PDI-Ph (21.77 mmol), and
Zn(OAc)2 (10.34 mmol) were dissolved in quinoline (50 mL) and heated at 130 °C for
12 h. After cooling to room temperature, the reaction mixture was poured into 2 N
HCl (200 mL). The resulting precipitate was filtered off, dried under vacuum, and
purified by column chromatography on silica gel, with eluent petroleum ether / DCM
= 1:1 to afford (S/R)-ClPDI-Ph as orange solids.
For the synthesis of (S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph, (S/R)-ClPDI-Ph (1.36 mmol), CuI (8.09
mmol), L-proline (9.43 mmol), and K2CO3 (14.83 mmol) were dissolved in DMSO
(25 mL) and heated at 75 °C under N2 for 10 h. After cooling, the mixture was
neutralized with 1 M HCl and then extracted with DCM. The organic layers were
separated, washed with brine, dried over Na2SO4, and purified by column
chromatography on silica gel, with eluent petroleum ether / DCM = 1:1 to afford
(S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph as black solids.
The (S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph crystals were obtained through a bottom-up solution-phase
self-assembly process. Specifically, (S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph (1.0 mg) was dissolved in
toluene (1.0 mL) in a 20 mL vial. Then, 2.0 mL of methanol was injected into the
solution. After several days, (S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph crystals were obtained.
1.2 Fabrication of Photodetectors


===== PDF PAGE 4 =====
S4
Heavily doped n-type silicon wafers (< 0.004 Ω · cm) with a 300 nm thermally grown
SiO2 dielectric layer (Ci = 10 nF cm−2) were used as substrates for devices. The
SiO2/Si wafers were cleaned with freshly prepared piranha solution (a mixture of 70
vol% H2SO4 and 30 vol% H2O2) for 1 h, followed by UV–ozone treatment. The
surface
of
the
wafers
was
treated
with
a
self-assembled
monolayer
of
n-octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTS). OTS solution (3 mM in trichloroethylene) was
spin-coated at 1500 rpm for 30 s onto the wafers. Then the samples were kept
overnight in a vacuum desiccator with a separate vial containing a few drops of
NH4OH. The wafers were sequentially washed with toluene, acetone, and isopropyl
alcohol, and dried with nitrogen blowing.
Single crystals of (R)- or (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph were drop-cast onto OTS-treated SiO2/Si
substrates and annealed at 70 °C in a vacuum to evaporate the remaining solvent. Ag
(60 nm) stripes were stamped onto the crystals on the SiO2/Si substrate, serving as the
source and drain electrodes.

1.5 Performance Metrics for Photodetectors
The responsivity (R) quantifies the electrical output per unit incident optical power
and is given by:
R=
Ilight-Idark
WincS
where Ilight and Idark are the photocurrent and dark current, respectively, Winc is the
incident light intensity, and S is the active area.
The specific detectivity (D*) measures the device’s ability to detect weak signals
and is dominated in organic systems by dark-current noise:
D*=
R S
2eIdark
where e is the elementary charge.
The external quantum efficiency (EQE) represents the ratio of the number of
collected charge carriers to the number of incident photons and is expressed as:
EQE=
Ilight-Idark hc
WincSeλ
= hcR
eλ
where h is Planck’s constant, c is the speed of light, and λ is the excitation
wavelength.
The rise (τr) and fall (τf) times correspond to the intervals during which the
photocurrent increases from 10% to 90% and decreases from 90% to 10%,
respectively, upon switching the illumination on and off.
1.6 Computational Methods


===== PDF PAGE 7 =====
S7
Structure optimization and a stepped plot are employed to probe stable conformations.
Calculations were performed with ORCA 6.0.[1,2] Three conformations were extracted,
corresponding respectively to the (RRRR)-MM, (SSSS)-MM configurations described
in the main text, and the configuration in the crystal. Molecular geometries were
optimized at the B3LYP-D3/def2-TZVP(-f) level of theory,[3–7] combined with the
SMD solvent model[8,9] (solvent: dichloromethane), with RI[10] and COSX[11,12]
acceleration
enabled,
and
single-point
energies
were
recalculated
at
the
ωB97M-V[13]/def2-TZVP level of theory, combined with the SMD solvent model
(solvent: dichloromethane), with RI and COSX acceleration enabled.
TDDFT calculations were conducted to generate simulated circular dichroism
spectra, using the crystal configuration mentioned above. TDDFT calculations were
performed at the PBE0[14]/def2-SV(P) level of theory, combined with the SMD
solvent model (solvent: dichloromethane), with RI and COSX acceleration enabled.
The simulated spectra agree well with the experimental spectra. Multiwfn[15] was used
to post-process and compute the charge density difference, which was visualized with
VMD.[16]
For periodic structure, single-point calculations were performed on the crystal
structure using CP2K2024.1[17,18] at the PBE[19]/DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH[20,21] level,
and the IGMH analysis[22] was conducted with Multiwfn.

2.5 Variable-temperature 1H NMR spectra of (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph


===== PDF PAGE 12 =====
S12
Figure S6. (a) Variable-temperature 1H NMR spectra (600 MHz, C2D2Cl4) recorded
for (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph. (b) Energy barrier for the transformation from the (M,M) to the
(P,M) configuration.
Backbone Stability (High-Temperature VT-NMR): VT-NMR experiments in C2D2Cl4
revealed no coalescence of resonances for the diastereomers in the lower-field region
between 293 K and 393 K, indicating a high barrier for P/M enantiomerization, which
remains slow even at elevated temperatures (Figure S6a). DFT calculations show that
the activation barrier for the transformation from the stable (M,M) configuration to the
intermediate (P,M) configuration is approximately 97.1 kJ mol1, explaining the lack
of coalescence in the VT-NMR data and confirming the kinetic stability of the helical
backbone (Figure S6b). Additionally, the mixed (P,M) configuration is 22.5 kJ mol1
higher in energy than the (M,M) global minimum, making the (P,M) isomer
thermodynamically unfavorable and absent in the spectra.
Figure S7. (a) Variable-temperature 1H NMR spectra (600 MHz, CD2Cl2) recorded
for (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph. (b) Energy barrier for rotation of a chiral side chain around the
amide bond.
Side-Chain Dynamics (Low-Temperature VT-NMR): In contrast to the rigid backbone,


===== PDF PAGE 13 =====
S13
the chiral side chains exhibit conformational freedom. As shown in Figure S7a, upon
cooling in CD2Cl2, the 1H NMR resonances in the high-field region begin to coalesce
around 223 K. This corresponds to the restricted rotation of the chiral 1-phenylethyl
substituent around the N–C imide bond (calculated barrier approximately 30–40 kJ
mol 1, Figure S7b). This distinction confirms that while the side chains are
dynamic,[23] the chiroptically active backbone is static.

2.7 Chiroptical Properties of (S)-tri-ClPDI-Ph
Figure S9. (a,b) Optimized molecular geometries of the two possible isomers
(straight and bent) of (S)-tri-ClPDI-Ph. (c) Calculated CD spectra comparing the
(S)-di-ClPDI-Ph (gray) and the (S)-tri-ClPDI-Ph isomers (blue/red), illustrating the
further redshift but diminishing returns in chiroptical intensity accumulation for the


===== PDF PAGE 14 =====
S14
trimer.
As shown in Figure S9, the tri-PDI system can theoretically adopt distinct
conformational isomers (straight and bent), with calculated CD spectra indicating that
the third PDI unit extends conjugation, causing a redshift into the infrared region. The
circular dichroism of the tri-PDI is also slightly higher than that of the di-PDI,
suggesting an accumulation of helicity through the CπE strategy. However,
synthesizing the trimer presents significant challenges, particularly in coupling
efficiency and stereochemical control, leading to a mixture of regioisomers that are
difficult to purify and crystallize.[24]
