Chiroπ-Extended Helitwistacene: Resolving the Planarity–Helicity
Dilemma for Broadband Circularly Polarized Light Detection
Xue Wang+, Ke Gao+, Siyuan Zhang+, Xianfeng Shen, Dong Zeng, Wenxuan Chang,
Huile Zhu, Xiaobo Shang,* Rongjin Li,* and Wenping Hu
Abstract: Achieving broadband and high-sensitivity circularly polarized light (CPL) detection with intrinsic organic
semiconductors remains a fundamental challenge due to the inherent planarity–helicity dilemma: conventional π-extension
strategies broaden absorption but tend to suppress molecular helicity, whereas enhanced helicity through steric modulation
often shortens conjugation and limits spectral coverage. Here, we introduce a Chiroπ-Extension (CπE) design strategy that
reconciles this conﬂict by bay-fusing two perylene diimides into a π-extended helitwistacene. This fusion simultaneously
elongates the conjugation pathway and enforces near-collinear alignment of electric and magnetic transition dipoles,
resulting in ampliﬁed chiroptical activity across the UV–visible region. Single-crystal devices of (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph exhibit
broadband CPL detection from 365 to 690 nm with an exceptional photocurrent dissymmetry factor of 0.60 at 515 nm,
along with high photoresponsivity (9.1 W−1) and detectivity (4.8 × 1012 Jones). This work establishes CπE as a general
molecular design principle for intrinsically chiral semiconductors, paving the way toward high-sensitivity, broadband, and
integrable CPL optoelectronic technologies.
Introduction
The direct detection of circularly polarized light (CPL) using
intrinsically chiral semiconductor materials eliminates the
need for external optical components such as polarizers
and quarter-wave plates, simplifying the optical conﬁgura-
tion and enabling both device miniaturization and on-chip
integration.[1–4] Broadband CPL detection, extending from
the ultraviolet (UV) to the visible or even near-infrared
(NIR) region is particularly promising for advanced applica-
tions in polarization imaging, secure optical communication,
and spin-selective photonic devices.[5–8]

right-handed CPL (RCPL) is typically quantiﬁed by the
absorption dissymmetry factor (gabs) and the photocurrent
dissymmetry factor (gph), which are deﬁned as:

Results and Discussion
Molecular Design, Synthesis, and Photophysical Properties
Chiral PDIs have long been recognized as versatile chro-
mophores for optoelectronic and chiroptical applications due
to their tunable molecular structures, high photostability,
and strong π–π stacking interactions.[23–26] To implement
the CπE design strategy, we synthesized (R)- and (S)-di-
ClPDI-Ph from their corresponding chiral monomers, (R)-
and (S)-ClPDI-Ph (Figure 1a). These monomers were synthe-
sized by reacting 1,6,7,12-Tetrachloroperylene tetracarboxylic
dianhydride with (R)- or (S)-1-phenylethylamine, followed
by L-proline-promoted CuI-catalyzed direct Ullmann C–C
coupling to yield the (R)- and (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph (Schemes
S1 and S2). The molecular structures were conﬁrmed by 1H
NMR, 13C NMR, variable-temperature NMR, and IR spectra
(see Supporting Information, Synthesis and Characterization,
Figures S1–S9).
The (R)- and (S)-1-phenylethyl substituents act as stere-
ochemical directors, inducing a preferred helical chirality in
the dimeric backbone and stabilizing one helical conformer
over the other. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
(Figure S10) show that the (RRRR)-MM conformation is
6.56 kJ mol−1 lower in energy than the (SSSS)-MM con-
formation, indicating that (S)-1-phenylethyl preferentially
induces P-helicity while (R)-1-phenylethyl favors M-helicity.
This side-chain–driven chiral induction effectively controls
the helical conﬁguration of the π-conjugated core while sim-
plifying synthesis, lowering production costs, and enhancing
the commercial viability for chiral organic semiconductors.
The resulting (R)- and (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph molecules embody
the core concept of the CπE molecular design strategy, which
integrates π-extension with helicity ampliﬁcation through
structural fusion. In this architecture, two PDI units are
covalently fused at their bay regions, forming a rigid
π-extended backbone that incorporates two helicene-like
substructures within a single conjugated framework. The bay-
fusion simultaneously extends the π-conjugation pathway and
introduces a helical twist across the fused backbone, gener-
ating a distinct helitwistacene geometry. This conﬁguration
represents a molecular embodiment of the planarity–helicity
balance central to the CπE concept.
The photophysical properties of these chiral molecules
were characterized by UV–vis absorption and circular
dichroism (CD) spectroscopy. As shown in Figure 1b, the
π-extension in (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph signiﬁcantly broadens the
absorption from 300 to 700 nm, compared with the 400–
550 nm range of the monomer (R)-ClPDI-Ph (1.0 × 10−5 M
in DCM). The monomers exhibit negligible Cotton effects,
conﬁrming that the chiral side chains alone do not impart
substantial helicity. In contrast, the dimers display strong
mirror-image CD signals spanning the entire UV–visible
region (Figure 1c), with |gabs| values reaching 8.6 × 10−3 at
502 nm (Figure 1d). These results clearly demonstrate that
π-extension via the CπE strategy enhances both chiroptical
activity and broadens the spectral coverage.
To elucidate the origin of these enhanced chiroptical
properties, time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) calculations were
performed based on the experimentally determined crystal
structure. The calculated CD spectra (Figure 1e) accurately
reproduce the experimental results, supporting the reliabil-
ity of the calculations. Charge density difference (CDD)
(Figure S11) reveals that the CD peaks near between 500
and 650 nm originate from localized excitations within the
fused PDI backbone, dominated by the S1 and S6 transitions.
The S1 and S6 excited states are primarily governed by
the HOMO→LUMO and HOMO→LUMO + 1 transitions,
respectively. The excitation at 400 nm is composed of several
transitions, representatively governed by the S14 and S17
excited states, and is attributed to charge transfer between
the fused PDI backbones and the imide carbonyl groups.
The LUMO and LUMO + 1 correspond to the molecular
LUMO states formed by different wavefunction combinations
of the monomer ClPDI marked Fragment A and Frag-
ment B, as described by the formula under the visualized
molecular orbitals (Figure 1f). Therefore, the long-wavelength
absorption peak arises from the entire dimeric π-conjugated
system constructed using the CπE design strategy, in which

We evaluated μ, m, and θ for (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph in its
HOMO→LUMO and HOMO→LUMO + 1 transitions
(Tables S2–S6). While the μ and m of the two fragments
are initially perpendicular to each other, the gabs values
of monomer (S)-ClPDI-Ph are nearly negligible, which is
consistent with experimental results. Nevertheless, upon
fusion of the two fragments, the resulting µsum and msum
become nearly collinear (Figure 1g; Figures S12 and S13).
Speciﬁcally, for both Fragment A and Fragment B in the
HOMO→LUMO transition, m is nearly perpendicular to μ.
However, when these fragments are fused into the dimer
(S)-di-ClPDI-Ph, msum becomes nearly collinear with µsum,
enhancing the dot product of µsum and msum and resulting
in high |gabs| values. Similarly, in the HOMO→LUMO + 1
transition, after fusing Fragment A and Fragment B, the
relative orientation of m and μ changes from perpendicular
to nearly collinear, also increasing the |gabs| values. The
π-extended helitwistacene framework, realized through the
CπE strategy, therefore enforces optimal transition dipole
alignment, simultaneously preserving and amplifying helicity
while extending conjugation. This synergy yields high |gabs|
values and broad spectral response—deﬁning the hallmark of
the CπE design concept.
Chiroptical Properties of Single Crystals
The performance of optoelectronic devices is strongly gov-
erned by the solid-state organization of their constituent
molecules, motivating the investigation of the morphology
and chiroptical properties of the CπE-engineered materials
in the single-crystal state. Single crystals of (R)- and (S)-di-
ClPDI-Ph were obtained at room temperature via the slow
diffusion of methanol into a toluene solution. Experimental
details and crystallization procedures are provided in the
Supporting Information.
Single-crystal
X-ray
diffraction
(SCXRD)
analysis
revealed
that
(S)-di-ClPDI-Ph
crystallizes
in
the
non-
centrosymmetric monoclinic space group P21 (Figure 2a),
conﬁrming its chiral crystal structure. The unit-cell parameters
were determined to be a = 11.500 (2°) Å, b = 35.601 (7°) Å,
c = 117.014 (4°) Å, and β = 110.432 (7°) (Crystallographic
data are presented in Table S7).[28] The π–π stacking distance
between adjacent molecular planes is 3.441 Å (Figure 2b),
indicative of efﬁcient orbital overlap, which is conducive
to efﬁcient charge transport. The dimeric PDI core adopts
an exclusive P-type helical conﬁguration, with a dihedral
angle of 40.88° between the two PDI subunits (Figure 2c),
consistent with the molecular conformation observed in the
monomeric state.
Both
enantiomers
form
well-deﬁned
parallelogram-

CD spectra of the single crystals revealed a maximum
|gabs| value of 6.2 × 10−3 at 518 nm for (R)- and (S)-

than that of the (R)- and (S)-ClPDI-Ph crystals (|gabs|

the π-extended helitwistacene exhibits stronger chiroptical
activity in the condensed phase. Through the CπE strategy,
planarity and helicity are harmonized within a single π-
framework: extended conjugation broadens absorption and
facilitates transport, while embedded helical twists preserve
strong dipole coupling.
This molecular synergy under-

Device Fabrication and Optoelectronic Performance
To
evaluate
the
optoelectronic
performance
and
CPL
detection capabilities of the π-extended helitwistacene,
two-terminal Ag/(S/R)-di-ClPDI-Ph/Ag photodetectors were
fabricated (Figure 3a). Beneﬁting from the CπE-induced π-
extension and optimized crystal packing, the (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph
device exhibited strong and broadband photoresponse across
the UV–visible range (365–690 nm; Figure 3b). The current–
voltage (I–V) characteristics measured in the dark and
under monochromatic illumination (Figure 3c; Figure S19)
demonstrate pronounced photocurrent generation, even at
low irradiance levels. Key performance metrics extracted
from these measurements (Figure 3d; Figure S20) reveal
outstanding device performance, with maximum external
quantum efﬁciency (EQE), R, and D* values of 1780.4%, 9.1
A W−1, and 4.8 × 1012 Jones, respectively (Table S8). These
values exceed those of most previously reported intrinsic
chiral small-molecule CPL detectors by one to two orders of
magnitude in both R and D* (Table S9).

photosensivity, fast response, long-term stability, and low
intrinsic noise underscores the potential of the CπE-based
system for high-performance polarization-resolved imaging.
The CPL discrimination ability was further examined by
measuring real-time photocurrent responses under LCPL and
RCPL at 515 nm (Figure 3g). The (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph crystal-
based device exhibited higher sensitivity to LCPL, generating
signiﬁcantly stronger photocurrent than under RCPL. The
corresponding gph, calculated using Equation (2), reached
a high value of 0.60 at 515 nm—ﬁvefold higher than that
of the monomer (S)-ClPDI-Ph (gph = 0.12; Figure S24).
Conversely, the (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph device showed the opposite
response, with higher sensitivity to RCPL and an average
gph = −0.57 (Figure S25). The large gph value are attributed
to the synergistic contribution of two mechanisms. First,
the intrinsically high |gabs| values of the chiral molecules
enhance selective CPL absorption, resulting in asymmetric
generation of photogenerated charge carriers. Second, the
chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) effect of the single crys-
tal promotes spin-dependent charge transport and collection
(Figure S26), further magnifying the CPL response.[31] For
comparison, the CPL performance of (R)-di-ClPDI-Ph thin-
ﬁlm devices was also examined, yielding a lower average gph
value of −0.11 (Figure S27). This reduced discrimination is
attributed to grain boundaries and increased structural dis-
order in the polycrystalline ﬁlms, which impair efﬁcient CPL
differentiation. Wavelength-dependent measurements further
conﬁrmed broadband CPL detection at 365, 450, 590, 635, and
690 nm (Figure 3h; Figures S28–S32). The (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph
crystal maintained high discrimination over the entire spectral
range, exhibiting average gph values of 0.14, −0.20, 0.35, 0.42,
and −0.18, respectively. Statistical analysis of six indepen-
dently fabricated devices conﬁrmed reproducible broadband
CPL detection with consistent performance (Figure S33).

RGB Circularly Polarized Imaging
Unlike conventional intensity-based imaging systems that
record only the light amplitude, CPL-resolved imaging
captures the polarization-dependent optical characteristics
of incident light through the determination of Stokes
parameters.[43–45] This multidimensional sensing capability
enables the visualization of optical anisotropy, surface texture,
and chiral reﬂectivity—features that remain invisible to
standard imaging techniques.[46,47]
The CπE-based photodetectors developed in this study
exhibit
distinct
CPL
sensitivity
across

full

natural reference for validating CPL-selective detection.

polarization-resolved imaging of complex chiral objects. This
multifunctional capability underscores the potential of CπE-
engineered semiconductors as foundational materials for
next-generation polarization-sensitive imaging and optical
sensing technologies.
Conclusion
In summary, we have developed a Chiroπ-Extension (CπE)
molecular design strategy that resolves the planarity–helicity
dilemma in chiral π-conjugated systems by bay-fusing two
PDI units into a π-extended helitwistacene. This architecture
couples extended conjugation with enforced helicity, yielding
near-collinear electric and magnetic dipole alignment and
strong, broadband chiroptical activity across the UV–visible
range. Devices based on (S)-di-ClPDI-Ph single crystals
achieve broadband CPL detection (365–690 nm) with an
outstanding gph of 0.60 at 515 nm, high R of 9.1 A W−1, and
D* of 4.8 × 1012 Jones, alongside remarkable stability and
low noise. The resulting broadband CPL sensitivity enables
RGB circularly polarized color imaging, demonstrating the
potential of CπE-engineered semiconductors as a general
platform for high-performance chiral optoelectronic and
polarization-resolved photonic devices.
