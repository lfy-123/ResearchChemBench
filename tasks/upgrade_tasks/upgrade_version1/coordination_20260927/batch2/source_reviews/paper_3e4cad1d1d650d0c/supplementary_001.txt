1. General Consideration: 
 
1.1 Reagent Information. All reactions were carried out in screw cap reaction tubes 
under anaerobic conditions, unless otherwise stated. All chemicals were purchased from 
Sigma Aldrich, Alfa Aesar, Bld-pharma, and TCI-India. Benzene and THF were dried 
using sodium metal and benzophenone. The DMSO was pre-dried by stirring it with 
calcium hydride for two days and then further dried by azeotropic distillation with dry 
benzene under high vacuum. Any other solvents were bought from commercial sources 
and were used without further purification. Silica gel (60–120 mesh), obtained from 
Merck, was used for column chromatography. A mixture of hexane and ethyl acetate 
was used as the eluent for column chromatographic separation.  
1.2 Analytical Information. All isolated compounds were characterized by 1H NMR, 
13C NMR spectroscopy. Unless otherwise stated, all Nuclear Magnetic Resonance 
spectra were recorded on a Bruker 400 MHz and 500 MHz instrument. NMR spectra 
are reported in parts per million (ppm) and were measured relative to the signals for 
residual deuterated solvent, unless otherwise stated. Chemical shifts were calibrated 
using residual undeuterated solvent as an internal reference (CDCl3: 7.26 ppm for 1H 
NMR, 77.16 ppm for 13C NMR; d6-DMSO: 1H NMR, 2.50 ppm and 13C NMR, 39.52 
ppm; C6D6: 1H NMR, 7.16 ppm). Multiplicity was indicated as follows: s (singlet), d 
(doublet), t (triplet), q (quartet), m (multiplet), dd (doublet of doublet), td (triplet of 
doublets), dt (doublet of triplets). The product yields during the reaction optimization 
were determined by NMR analysis using ferrocene as an internal standard. Two white 
light LED (400-800 nm, 30 W) were used as the light source for photochemical 
reactions. High-resolution mass spectra (HRMS) were recorded on a Waters QTOF 
mass spectrometer. Cyclic voltammetry was performed using an AUTOLAB 
potentiostat. For all spectroscopic measurements, the solution was prepared in a 
nitrogen-filled glove box. UV-Vis spectral measurements were carried out with Agilent 
Cary 60 UV-Vis spectrophotometer using a quartz cuvette having a path length of 1 cm. 
The solution of anions was prepared inside a N2-filled glove box. All the steady-state 
fluorescence and excitation measurements were performed on a Fluoromax-4 
spectrofluorometer (Horiba Jobin Yvon, NJ) in 10 × 10 mm cuvettes. Fluorescence 
decays have been recorded on a (Horiba Jobin Yvon, NJ) Time-Correlated Single 
Photon Counting (TCSPC) in a 10 × 10 mm cuvette setup, where a hybrid

1.4 Intermittent Illumination Experimental Set-Up: Pulsed irradiation 
experiments were conducted using an Arduino Uno microcontroller as the central control unit. 
The Arduino was programmed using the Arduino Integrated Development Environment (IDE, 
version 1.8.18) to generate predefined illumination ON/OFF sequences with precise temporal 
control. A metal-cased switching power supply (ME-960W: AC Input-110/22V; DC output-
48V 20A) provided a stable external power source for the light-emitting diode (LED), ensuring 
constant electrical output during illumination periods. A white LED was used as the light 
source and was electrically isolated from the Arduino via a switching circuit, ensuring the LED 
was fully activated during ON periods and completely deactivated during OFF periods. 
Wireless communication was established between the control unit and the illumination module 
using an nRF24L01 wireless transceiver. The Arduino communicated with the nRF24L01 
module via the serial peripheral interface (SPI), enabling remote triggering and synchronization 
of the irradiation cycles without the need for direct wired connections. Illumination duty cycles 
of 1:1 were implemented by adjusting the programmed OFF time relative to a fixed ON time. 
Pulsed irradiation was employed to probe whether productive reactivity persists during dark 
periods, thereby distinguishing photo-controlled from chain-propagated pathways.

2. Catalyst synthesis: 
(a) Synthesis of aryl-substituted fluorene: 
 
 
 
Procedure: 
  
All 9-aryl-9H-fluorene derivatives were synthesized following a literature procedure after 
some modification.1 In an oven-dried, argon-purged round-bottom flask, the Grignard solution 
was prepared from magnesium turnings (15 mmol) reacting with aryl-bromo derivatives (10 
mmol) in anhydrous THF (30 mL). In another Schlenk flask, fluorenone (8 mmol) was 
dissolved in anhydrous THF (15 mL), cooled to 0°C, and then the freshly prepared Grignard 
solution was added dropwise to the chilled fluorenone solution over a period of 20 minutes. 
The mixture was then allowed to come to room temperature and stirred for 24 hours. After 
completion, the reaction was quenched by the addition of saturated aqueous ammonium 
chloride (40 mL) and then extracted three times with dichloromethane (3 × 40 mL). The 
combined organic layers were dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, and the solvent was evaporated 
under reduced pressure. The residue was then purified by flash column chromatography on 
silica gel to yield Fluoren-9-ol as a colorless liquid. 
In the subsequent step, the liquid was transferred into a 100 mL round-bottom flask and 
dissolved in 30 mL of DCM. To this solution, 1.5 equivalents of triethyl silane and 1.6 
equivalents of trifluoroacetic acid were added. The solution was stirred for 30 mins at room 
temperature. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by the addition of water and 
extracted three times with dichloromethane. The combined organic layers were dried over 
anhydrous Na2SO4, and the solvent was evaporated under reduced pressure. The product was 
purified by column chromatography using hexane as an eluent. The products were obtained as

ip = 0.4463nFAC(nFD/RT)1/2 
where 
ip = peak current 
n = no of electrons transferred in the redox event 
A = electrode surface area 
C = bulk concentration of analyte 
D = diffusion coefficient of analyte 
 = scan rate 
The peak current (ip) is plotted against the square root of scan rate ()1/2 giving a linear

11a. Fluorescence quenching experiment and Stern-Volmer plot: 
 
All the steady-state fluorescence measurements were performed on a Fluoromax-4 
spectrofluorometer (Horiba Jobin Yvon, NJ) in 10 × 10 mm cuvettes. These experiments were 
performed using 5 ×10-5 M solution of 1a- (prepared by the addition of 1.3 equiv of KOtBu as 
a base) in dry DMSO, upon addition of the appropriate amount of quencher (4-chloro anisole) 
in each run. The fluorescence intensity was traced in a spectrofluorometer, keeping the analyte 
solution in a quartz cuvette. The solutions were prepared in a nitrogen-filled glove box. The 
solution was irradiated at 373 nm, and the emission intensity was examined at 547 nm. As the 
concentration of the quencher was increased, a gradual decrease in fluorescence intensity was 
observed, which was plotted with respect to the increasing quencher (4-chloroanisole) 


===== PDF PAGE 24 =====
24 
 
concentration. The obtained plot followed the Stern-Volmer pattern, showing a Ksv value of 
86540 M-1.  
Stern-Volmer equation is the following: 
 
I0/I = 1 + KSV[Q] 
where 
I0 = the fluorescence intensity without the quencher 
I = fluorescence intensity in the presence of the quencher 
[Q] = concentration of added quencher 
KSV = Stern-Volmer quenching constant

17d. C-C cross-coupling using 2,6-dimethyl chlorobenzene: 
 
This control reaction was performed to refute the involvement of benzyne mechanism for the 
cross-coupling reaction. Inside a glove box, a raection tube was charged with 2,6-dimethyl 
chlorobenzene (0.5 mmol), KOtBu (1.5 mmol), and 1a (10 mol%). Dry benzene (5 mL) was 
added to the reaction mixture, and the tube was tightly closed. The reaction mixture was stirred 
under visible light irradiation for 24 h at room temperature. After the completion of the 
reaction, the solvent was removed under vacuum, and the product was purified using column 
chromatography using hexane as eluent, which afforded C (8%) and D (22%) in an overall 30% 
yield. Meta-xylene was a volatile liquid and its formation we were unable to quantify. The 
formation of product C and the biphenyl D strongly argue against the involvement of benzyne 
mechanism. The yield is typically low for the steric hindrance at the coupling site.  
 
17e. Role of potassium counteraction for C-C cross-coupling reaction: 
 
When KOtBu is used to deprotonate 9-phenylfluorene molecules, potassium remains as the 
counter-cation. We were curious to know whether this cation has any special role in catalysis, 
especially in substrate activation. To achieve this goal, a crown ether, 18-C-6, was used in the 
reaction medium to encapsulate potassium. A model cross-coupling reaction was performed 
using 4-chloroanisole as the substrate and 1a as the catalyst, in benzene (with a higher excess 
used as the solvent), while keeping all other conditions constant. In the presence of 18-C-6, the 
desired cross-coupled product formed in 70% yield. This slight drop in yield compared to the 
regular condition may be indicative of a role for potassium. We allude to the potassium-driven 
non-covalent interaction between the fluorene anion and the substrate. However, the role is 
rather subtle, and its encapsulation does not significantly alter the reaction outcome.

21. General procedure for hydrodehalogenation of aryl chlorides: 
 
 
 
An oven-dried 20 mL screw capped reaction tube, equipped with a magnetic stir bar was taken 
inside N2 filled glove box. Inside the glove box, the reaction tube was charged with 0.5 mmol 
of aryl halides, 10 mol % of photocatalyst 1a, 2 equiv of γ-terpinene, and 3 equiv of KOtBu, 
followed by the addition of 5 mL of dry DMSO as solvent. The cap was closed tightly, and the 
reaction tube was taken out of the glove-box followed by sealing the cap with parafilm. The 
reaction mixture was irradiated with two white LEDs (30 W each) for 12 hours with continuous 
stirring. At the end of the reaction, the reaction mixture was extracted using ice-cold water and 
ethyl acetate. The organic layer was evaporated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The 
residue was purified by column chromatography using hexane as an eluent to obtain the pure 
product. The desired products were fully characterized by 1H, 13C NMR spectroscopies. In the 
case of volatile products, the product yield was calculated using 1H NMR spectroscopy using 
trimethoxy benzene as an internal standard.

22. General procedure for the synthesis of C-C cross-coupling reaction: 
 
 
                
An oven-dried 20 mL screw-capped reaction tube, equipped with a magnetic stir bar was taken 
inside N2 filled glove box. Inside the glove-box the reaction tube was charged with 0.5 mmol 
of substituted chlorobenzene derivatives, 10 mol % of photocatalyst 1a and 3 equiv of KOtBu 
followed by the addition of 5 mL of dry benzene. The cap was closed tightly and the reaction 
tube was taken out of the glove-box followed by sealing the cap with parafilm. The reaction 
mixture was irradiated with two white LEDs (30 W each) for 24 hours with continuous stirring. 
The distance of the reaction tube from the lamp was 7-8 cm. Under this condition, the photon 
flux on the reaction tube was 25.1 mW/cm2.  At the end of the reaction, the reaction mixture 
was evaporated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The residue was purified by column 
chromatography using hexane as an eluent to obtain the pure product. The isolated products 
were fully characterized by 1H, 13C NMR spectroscopies. 
 
23. Synthesis of C-C cross-coupled product using heteroarene and 
phosphonates as coupling partners: 
 
 
 
An oven-dried 15 mL screw capped reaction tube, equipped with a magnetic stir bar was taken 
inside N2 filled glove-box. Inside the glove box, the reaction tube was charged with 0.5 mmol 
of substituted chlorobenzene derivatives, 10 mol % of photocatalyst 1a, and 3 equiv of KOtBu 
followed by the addition of 1 mL of thiophene/ furan/ N-methyl pyrrole/tri-methyl 
phosphonoacetate (2 equiv) in 4 mL dry DMSO. The cap was closed tightly, and the reaction 
tube was taken out of the glove-box, followed by sealing the cap with parafilm. The reaction 
mixture was irradiated with two white LEDs (30 W each) for 24 hours with continuous stirring.

The reaction mixture was then extracted using ice-cold water and ethyl acetate. The organic 
layer was evaporated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The residue was purified by column 
chromatography using hexane as an eluent to obtain the pure product. The desired products 
were fully characterized by 1H, 13C NMR spectroscopy. In a separate reaction, instead of 1 mL 
thiophene, 20 equivalents were used, and we found decreased yield of the cross-coupled 
product.  
 
24. General procedure for borylation of aryl chloride: 
 
 
 
An oven-dried 15 mL screw capped reaction tube, equipped with a magnetic stir bar was taken 
inside N2 filled glove-box. Inside the glove-box the reaction tube was charged with 0.5 mmol 
of aryl halide or phenyl phosphonate, 5 mol % of photocatalyst 1a, 3 equiv of Cs2CO3 and 3 
equiv of B2Pin2, followed by the addition of 5 mL of dry DMSO. The cap was closed tightly 
and the reaction tube was taken out of the glove-box, followed by sealing the cap with parafilm. 
The reaction mixture was irradiated with two white LEDs (30 W each) for 24-36 hours with 
continuous stirring. The reaction mixture was extracted using ice-cold water and ethyl acetate. 
The organic layer was evaporated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The residue was 
purified by column chromatography using hexane and ethyl acetate as eluents to obtain the 
pure product. The desired products were fully characterized by 1H, 13C NMR spectroscopies.
