## Scientific objective

What molecular factors can account for the contrasting benzoate-substitution outcomes and electrochemical behavior of the seven specified alpha-carbonyl TEMPO-derived alkoxyamines, and how do carbonyl functionality, alpha-carbon substitution and side-chain structure influence productive substitution versus unproductive reaction under the stated experimental conditions?

Develop and assess an evidence-supported molecular explanation, not merely a restatement of the observations. Productive reaction means formation of the specified benzoate connectivity. An exact yield or rate prediction, a complete reaction network and a stereochemical prediction are not requested.

## Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/system.json`. PI1-PI7 identify the neutral molecular graphs; PI8 describes product connectivity, reaction conditions and separate voltammetry measurements; PI9-PI12 give the observed outcomes. The names specify connectivity without imposing an unreported absolute configuration or enantiopurity. Build the structures and any additional states required by your chosen model independently.

The scientific comparisons are carbonyl functionality across the secondary substrates, the methyl/butyl side-chain contrast within the Weinreb amides, and the primary/secondary ketone contrast, considered together with the electrochemical observations. The primary ketone's yield comes from different DCM screening conditions, not a matched MeCN control. Distinguish isolated and crude-NMR yields, half-wave and irreversible half-peak potentials, and nondetection from a measured exact zero. The potential measurements are separate experiments; they are not imposed electrolysis potentials. Do not assume that product yields are intrinsic rates or that reversibility alone identifies a chemical pathway. No additional substrate, catalyst, nucleophile survey, unmeasured product or experimental campaign is required.

## Required investigation

Formulate testable explanations relevant to these connected comparisons and choose computational models and informative investigations that can distinguish or constrain them. Actually execute the research: preserve the inputs, outputs, logs and analysis that support each claimed observation or decision. Explain model choices, map chemical identities and states to the public inputs, and identify limitations that affect interpretation. Choose the physical effects, observables, structures and level of theory yourself; no particular descriptor, intermediate, hypothesis count, test count or search sequence is mandatory.

Use the evidence to retain, revise or reject explanations, and distinguish what your calculations establish from plausible interpretations. Address all three scientific comparisons and their relationship to the voltammetric behavior; a standalone structure or single computed number does not answer the question. Partial or unsuccessful investigations may leave some comparisons unresolved if their evidence and limitations are reported honestly. No exhaustive conformer/pathway search or proof of a complete mechanism is required.

Validation follows the claims actually made. Check molecular identity, state definitions, units and computational reliability. If you claim a minimum or transition state, provide appropriate stationary-point validation; a transition-state connectivity claim also needs suitable connection evidence. If you introduce a kinetic model, provide its equations, mapping to observations, assumptions and identifiability limits. These are conditional requirements, not a requirement to use transition states, kinetics or any particular method.

If an author-provided scientific guidance block is publicly supplied, execute and critically assess its disclosed computational baseline within this same question, recording fidelity, ambiguities and justified substitutions in investigation records and the report. Otherwise choose the research approach independently; undisclosed methods and optional author interpretations are not requirements. In either case, agreement with a reference alone is insufficient, and well-supported alternative conclusions are acceptable.

## Deliverables and stopping conditions

Submit `report/results.json`, `report/report.md` and the actual research artifacts under `research/`, following `submission_schema.json` and `submission_guide.md`. Use the guide's stable IDs, evidence/conclusion links, artifact index and execution-status conventions. The report must give the explanatory comparison, the actual investigations and outcomes, evidence-based decisions, model/condition boundaries, uncertainty, and actual resource use.

Stop when the executed evidence supports the conclusions you claim and the remaining uncertainties are stated. There is no fixed CPU, GPU, memory, walltime or job-count quota. Research completion and scientific question resolution are distinct: complete research can leave the question unresolved, and honest partial work or execution failure is acceptable with attempted inputs, real logs and a defensible account of the limitations. Do not claim resolution merely from a status label, a plausible mechanism or reproduction of a published value.

The shared assessment allocates 60 points to research quality (testable explanations, informative models/tests, reliable execution, evidence-based decisions, reproducibility and calibrated limitations) and 40 points to resolution of the connected carbonyl-function, side-chain and primary/secondary comparisons. Unresolved work can earn research-quality credit, but not automatic question-resolution credit. Optional comparison analyses carry no separate score or omission penalty.
