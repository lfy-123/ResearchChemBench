## Scientific objective

How does the specified visible-light reaction of buta-1,3-diene, sodium difluoromethanesulfinate and methyl 2,3,4,5,6-pentafluorobenzoate form the observed polyfluoroaryl sulfones, and what governs its 1,4-versus-1,2 regiochemical competition and E/Z product preference, including the switch to preferential 1,2 addition for (1E)-1-phenylbuta-1,3-diene under its specified related conditions?

Develop an evidence-supported explanation of the connected product-forming chemistry and the linked selectivity comparisons, not just a characterization of one species or one energy. The observed structures and product preferences are starting facts to explain, not evidence that any proposed explanation is correct.

## Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Use `data/inputs/system.json`. PI1-PI3 identify the common starting molecules; PI4 specifies the distinct conditions and photocatalyst; PI5 gives the butadiene products and observations; PI6 identifies the terminal-phenyl comparison diene; PI7 gives its observed product. These are all the supplied scientific inputs.

For net addition labels, number the diene chain from the terminal CH2 end; in the phenyl diene the phenyl-bearing carbon is then C4. The 1,2 and 1,4 labels describe attachment positions in the products and do not prescribe a reaction sequence. Product alkene geometry is specified, but no absolute configuration of the phenyl product's sulfur-bearing carbon is supplied.

The question concerns the molecular product-forming chemistry of these systems and its regio-/stereochemical competition. Relate a connected account of the starting-to-product bond changes and chemical feasibility to the butadiene preference, its E/Z outcome and the phenyl-system reversal. A detailed photocatalyst excitation model, ion-exchange kinetics, a full solution-mixture speciation model, a global reaction-rate model and other substrate-library reactions are not required outcomes. Choose and state defensible model boundaries instead of treating omitted environmental effects as established facts.

The phenyl conditions are not a controlled substituent-only comparison with the butadiene conditions. Isolated yields are not calibrated rate constants or equilibrium populations; no quantitative byproduct detection limit is supplied. Do not infer an exact kinetic ratio, an unreported parameter or a numerical uncertainty from these observations alone.
