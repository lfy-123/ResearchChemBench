## Scientific objective

For the specified homogeneous palladium-catalyzed Heck arylation of 1,2-dihydronaphthalene with 4-tert-butylphenyl iodide, what accounts for the condition-dependent β-versus-α regioselectivity, and how are access to productive catalytic states and the relative energetics of competing product-forming routes connected to substrate-dependent reactivity when styrene is used as a model-olefin comparison?

Develop an evidence-supported account of this single connected reactivity and regioselectivity problem. The supplied product outcomes are observations to explain, not a mechanism to assume. Determine which conclusions your actual investigation supports and which remain uncertain.

## Public inputs and scientific boundaries

Read `data/inputs/system.json`. It defines the primary olefin and aryl iodide graphs, the dippf ligand and other starting reagents, the two reaction-condition sets, the ordinary α/β product connectivities and observations, and styrene as a model-olefin comparison. The β benchmark gives 91% yield and β/α = 45:1; the same-reactant α comparison gives approximately 60% combined product yield and α/β = 7:1. These yield conventions differ and are not absolute-rate measurements. No experimental styrene benchmark is provided.

Keep the primary experimental identities fixed. Choose and justify any model reduction, chemical state, solvent/temperature treatment or other approximation, and clearly distinguish model conclusions from experimental conclusions. No initial conformation, optimized structure, catalyst constitution or product-forming sequence is supplied. Do not silently replace the primary olefin, aryl group or ligand with another chemical system.

The scientific scope connects access to states capable of productive reaction, the relative structural/energetic basis of α/β product-forming competition under the specified conditions, and substrate dependence assessed using the styrene model comparison. It does not require a complete catalytic network, an absolute turnover-rate fit, a new experimental study, asymmetric reaction predictions or other substrate-scope reactions. No exhaustive conformer/pathway enumeration or particular explanatory descriptor is prescribed.
