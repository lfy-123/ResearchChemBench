Core scored statements and expected answers deliberately do not append the author's preferred contact, ring, descriptor, intermediate or mechanism. The observed 63% isolated yield and 96.5:3.5 enantiomer ratio of the optimized monoamination are experimental (`ev_doc_4b5d1c8b6138_000185_6efcbb975e67`, `ev_doc_e1783ea73595_000055_ca0c1a8f009b`), as is the increase in product ee while diamination product accumulates (`ev_doc_4b5d1c8b6138_000438_52c80a091837`). They are not computed enantiomer-ratio or kinetic references. The public view needs only the ordinary product identity and qualitative observed selectivity/time context because quantitative prediction is not a task goal.

The proposed dual hydrogen-bond catalytic activation, dearomatized INT-A and subsequent aromatization are author hypotheses. The discussion explicitly acknowledges a plausible alternative in which the other aryl ring engages the axle interaction and the less hindered ring reacts. Source wording says the charge comparison may favor a proposal, not that it establishes a unique mechanism. The original diagram was viewed so that a proposed transition-state drawing is not mistaken for a computed structure. No computed reaction TS, reaction-energy table or exchange/kinetic pathway is reported in the inspected substrate computational section.

## Limits preserved

Direct SI access resolves the frozen screening note about a missing Table S2 and reveals the NPA method, but those historical reference-record warnings are not rewritten. The source method is SMD toluene whereas the experiment is in chloroform. No model invariance is assumed. The inspected computational section supplies coordinates and population-charge results, but not frequency eigenvalues, numerical charge uncertainty, a conformer search-coverage record, populations, or reaction/exchange barriers. Lack of these nonessential author details does not make a graph-defined substrate study unstartable; they limit what can be validated against the author's conditional account. No missing author input deck or catalyst geometry is invented. Solver claims are instead assessed using the solver's own executed evidence and appropriate claim-dependent validation.
