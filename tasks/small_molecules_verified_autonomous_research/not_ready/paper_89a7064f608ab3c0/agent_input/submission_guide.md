# Reproducible evidence submission

Use agent-defined IDs for models, operations, artifacts, quantitative results, claims and validations. Record each model's complete molecular identity, charge and electronic state, relevant stereochemistry, composition, conditions and relation to the public objects in `data/inputs/reaction_inventory.json`. For catalyst models distinguish the coordination cation from its counterion and document any justified simplification. The supplied two-dimensional catalyst graphs specify connectivity, not optimized coordination geometries.

Link every quantitative claim to actual calculation inputs, native outputs and an extraction location. Preserve generated evidence under `outputs/` and analysis scripts under `code/`. General reagent/scientific/software references may be used with citations. The target article/SI, private evaluator and archived task solutions are outside the authorized public input. Supplied experimental observations and reagent properties are input evidence, not newly computed results.

Use `complete`, `partial`, `bounded_failure` or `input_blocked` truthfully. Partial and failed submissions may have empty result arrays when no physical result was obtained. Failed or blocked work requires `failure_report` with affected scope, cause and evidence. Do not invent values or connections to fill the schema. Missing scientific findings receive no corresponding result credit; process is assessed independently.

For claimed minima, saddles and states, provide the evidence needed to establish their identity and validity. A mechanistic connection requires a genuine connection test. A scan maximum, normal exit or unconnected imaginary mode is not itself a validated barrier. Use balanced common references for comparisons; distinguish electronic energies, thermochemical energies and local activation barriers. Different-charge total energies cannot be subtracted as if they described a balanced reaction without an electron reference. State electrochemical signs, electrode references and solvent conventions whenever applicable, and excitation-state and energy definitions for any excited-state quantities you elect to use.

Report molecular reaction free energies in kcal/mol. For comparison of local solution-phase reaction profiles use 298 K and a consistent 1 atm thermochemical convention, stating any conversion or difference from a native default. Room temperature is the experimental description, not a measured 298 K datum. Other quantities retain their physically appropriate units and fully documented conventions. Do not mix energies or thermal corrections from unmatched geometries, states or methods.

Methods, model choices and exploration are yours. No fixed hypothesis count, named intermediate sequence, calculation count or mandatory property list is imposed. Establish the activation and framework interpretation using actual discriminating evidence. Electron and atom accounting is required; calculating every elementary step of a catalyst cycle is not.
