## Item-by-item criteria

### r_competing_addition_channels

Actual structural and reaction evidence supports three candidate methanol-involving C–O bond-forming channels, mapped to the major, minor and comparator connectivities, respectively. The authors' three TSs for the same cyclopropenium/methanol system serve as the reference; equivalent state representations for the same scientific comparison are acceptable. Any claimed TS must have the target bond-forming mode and appropriate connectivity evidence; three product energies alone cannot replace formation channels.

### r_major_vs_minor_formation

Actual calculations with the same composition, the same charge and compatible energy zeros support a more favorable formation channel for major than for minor. The authors' TS1′−TS1=1.3 kcal/mol provides context; the ordering must be correct, and numerical proximity or a higher experimental major yield alone cannot substitute for evidence of competition.

### r_major_vs_comparator_formation

Actual comparable calculations support a lower-energy formation channel leading to major than to comparator. The authors' TS1″−TS1=7.1 kcal/mol provides context only; this relative TS difference must not be called an absolute barrier. Exact numerical agreement is not required.

### r_major_vs_minor_stability

Among the three specified products, actual valid minima and compatible G comparisons support major being lower in energy than minor. The authors' minor−major=3.2 kcal/mol serves as a reference; comparisons must use the same composition and physical quantity, and charged addition states cannot replace neutral products.

### r_minor_vs_comparator_stability

Actual neutral-product G comparisons support minor being lower in energy than comparator, which, together with major being the lowest, establishes the ordering major<minor<comparator. The authors' values of 3.2/7.2 kcal/mol relative to major provide context; no credit is awarded without calculations or for reversed ordering.

### r_selectivity_answer

The final answer integrates actual formation-channel comparisons and neutral-product stability to establish that major forms more readily and is more stable in the model studied, using both lines of evidence to explain the supplied regioselectivity. Distinguish unfavorable comparator formation/stability from a strictly zero yield. Hydrogen-bonding/steric causation that has not been independently decomposed is not a mandatory answer, and a pathway must not be inferred solely from a low product energy.

### r_redistribution_answer

Use actual neutral-product G comparisons to determine that the thermodynamic redistribution direction from P3 toward P2 is favorable, and distinguish this from competition in initial formation. Reproducing undisclosed experimental endpoints is not required. Product G alone must not be used to claim that complete exchange kinetics, full equilibrium or rates have been calculated.

## Package changes and reference boundaries

Public major/minor product rankings, yields and the directional redistribution observation are removed and replaced by three unranked candidates. Privately, P2=major, P3=minor and P1=comparator. The original scientific propositions and weights are retained; the redistribution item now predicts the thermodynamic direction and does not require restating an experiment that was not supplied.
