## Item-by-item criteria

### r_sensitized_triplet

Actual calculations support an alkene-associated triplet/open-shell excited state of this substrate whose energy is compatible with TXO sensitization conditions and which can serve as the reactive starting point for skeletal rearrangement. Compare compatible excitation-energy definitions, electronic states and solvents. The paper's 52.2 is a Gibbs energy difference between states, not a vertical electronic excitation energy; TXO 65.5 is a literature reference, not an actual calculation from this verification. Neither number has a scoring tolerance. The lamp wavelength alone or repetition of the authors' mechanism does not earn credit. An additional calculation of the carbonyl triplet at 75.6 to exclude a Norrish pathway is not required.

### r_addition_connectivity

Calculated structures/pathways of this substrate support intramolecular C–C addition of the alkene moiety to the carbonyl carbon to form the corresponding bicyclic skeletal intermediate, described by the authors as triplet 5-exo-trig addition. Actual local connectivity/reaction-mode evidence for this bond-forming event and a compatible electronic state is required; the product observation cannot replace it. Equivalent methods and labels are allowed; a full IRC, specific software or the exact value 9.7 is not mandatory.

### r_ring_opening

Actual structures/local pathways support cleavage of a C–C bond of the original ring after addition, yielding a ring-expanded open-shell intermediate leading to the target [5.1.0] skeleton, corresponding to the authors' eight-membered-ring diradical 3B. Assess this bond-cleavage and ring-expansion structural event independently of the preceding bond-formation item. The source local barrier of 3.0 and downhill reaction free energy of −13.6 are nonmandatory context. Do not mislabel a cumulative profile height as a local barrier; no thermochemical numerical threshold is added to this structural item.

### r_cis_preference

For cis/trans comparisons with the same target-product connectivity graph, the same conditions and compatible physical quantities, calculations support a lower free energy for the cis product, with the C1 methoxycarbonyl and C7 phenyl groups on the same face, than for trans. The two relative configurations must actually be distinguished; do not infer the result backward from the known cis observation. The paper's 23.5 is a Gibbs energy difference, whereas the approximately 3.7 difference at the crossing points is an electronic energy difference; they must not be mixed. Crossing points may provide additional relevant evidence, but a new MECP trajectory or crossing-point numerical threshold is not mandatory. The product stability difference itself is neither a room-temperature isomerization rate nor a precise dr.

### r_transformation_answer

Finally, use bond-forming/bond-breaking events and electronic-state changes supported by actual calculations to explain conversion of the given substrate to the given cyclopropane-fused [5.1.0] product through a bounded photosensitized open-shell addition–ring-expansion–ring-closure pathway. Integrate the intermediate local results with the correct target connectivity and DMSO/TXO irradiation conditions. Merely listing intermediates or restating the product does not earn credit. Equivalent nomenclature and equivalent physical explanations supported by actual evidence are acceptable; an arbitrary different mechanism is not automatically equivalent to the paper's result. Proof of a unique mechanism, ISC rates, all competing channels or the 99% yield is not required.

### r_stereochemistry_answer

Finally, explicitly connect the calculated preference for cis over trans in this system to the observed same-face relationship of the C1 methoxycarbonyl/C7 phenyl groups, providing a bounded energetic/structural explanation. Distinguish the thermodynamic product preference from possible selectivity factors involving ring closure/crossing. Merely stating that cis is more stable without applying the evidence to the given stereochemical result is insufficient. The lower cis crossing point may be incorporated but need not be the sole cause. Without a valid rate/kinetic model, do not directly equate the energy difference with precise selectivity or a room-temperature isomerization rate, or treat the 99% isolated yield as dr. Thermal experiments on other products 22/27 are not direct kinetic evidence for this product.
